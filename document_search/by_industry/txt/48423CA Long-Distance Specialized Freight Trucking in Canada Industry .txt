Long-Distance Specialized Freight Trucking in Canada
About This Industry
Definition The Tank and Refrigeration Trucking industry in Canada provides long-distance trucking services for atypical cargo requiring specialized transportation. Companies in this industry transport bulk liquids, frozen goods, dry bulk materials, forest products, automobiles and other specialized freight between metropolitan areas. However, this industry does not include the transport of used goods.

Executive Summary Long haul: Industry revenue will benefit from stable and recovering demand from downstream customers
The trucking industry navigated a challenging economic landscape. Companies grappled with economic volatility fueled by inflationary pressures, tightened monetary policy and rising fuel costs, which complicated the landscape, particularly for long-distance trucking companies competing against more efficient transport modes like intermodal and rail. Despite these hurdles, the industry benefited by offering flexible delivery services tailored to a diverse range of industries, from manufacturing and wholesale to perishable goods. Revenue is expected to increase at a CAGR of 0.5% to $16.6 billion through the end of 2024, including growth of 0.5% in 2024 alone.
Elevated borrowing costs have tempered broader consumption and distribution, pressuring markets like manufacturing, nonresidential construction and aggregate trade growth. Trucking companies have focused on improving driver safety, with companies investing in fleets equipped with modern technology like cargo sensors and digital dashboards, resulting in reductions in accidents and related costs. The industry's move towards electrification encounters hurdles, particularly around battery weight and inadequate charging infrastructure, posing challenges to truck capacity and revenue per trip. The growing integration of sensors and more fuel-efficient engines raises the cost of new trucks, pressuring profit.
Monetary easing is expected to lower borrowing costs, bolster economic activity and support a resurgence in downstream markets. As economic conditions improve and consumer spending rises, manufacturing and retail activities are projected to climb, heightening demand for transportation services. Technological advancements in remote monitoring systems and the gradual introduction of autonomous trucks will continue to reshape long-distance logistics, enhancing efficiency and reducing driving restrictions. Meanwhile, government and industry initiatives to improve driver retention and work-life balance, combined with streamlined immigration processes, are set to alleviate workforce shortages. Industry revenue is set to expand by a CAGR of 0.9% to an estimated $17.5 billion through the end of 2029.

What's driving current industry performance?
The industry's heavy saturation dipped momentarily
•
As revenue failed to immediately return to growth in the aftermath of COVID-19, some trucking companies weren't able to weather the challenges and were forced to close down permanently.
•
In the two years following the pandemic, the total number of tank and refrigeration enterprises fell for the first time in over a decade. This temporary alleviation of the industry's heavy saturation is partially responsible for a surge in revenue in 2022 as the challenges of the pandemic finally subsided and price competition lessened.
•
The industry's minimal barriers to entry are set to enable the total number of tank and refrigeration trucking companies to begin climbing again in 2023.
Economic volatility and rising fuel costs dampened performance
•
Inflationary pressures, tightened monetary policy and ongoing policy rate adjustments have driven volatility, preventing the economy from stabilizing. Economic activity dropped in 2020, leading to sluggish performance through 2024, as many downstream markets struggled to fully recover and offset the initial decline.
•
Rising oil costs disproportionately affect long-distance trucking distribution because it competes with intermodal and rail transport, which are more efficient and cheaper when transporting bulk goods over long distances. Trucking establishments are benefiting by offering flexible delivery services for a diversified range of industries, sheltering them from a decline in any particular market.
A decline in borrowing costs is offering relief for slowing growth
•
Falling inflation and the ongoing easing of the policy rate are supporting downstream industries, including the manufacturing, wholesale, retail and agricultural markets, revitalizing growth and setting up the industry for a strong start to 2025.
•
Many downstream markets depend on borrowing to finance the purchases of equipment and bulk shipments for their operations. 2021 and 2022 were two single best years in the period, driven by the release of pent-up demand and favorable borrowing conditions, which allowed downstream markets to secure financing for expansion.
•
The entire 2024 was subject to ripple effects of monetary tightening from 2023 as elevated borrowing costs started taking effect and dampening broader consumption and distribution. The 2.1% decline in nonresidential construction and slowing trade growth to 0.7% pressured the need for distributing specialized freight and continued weighing on growth.

Businesses are prioritizing the safety of the drivers during long-haul routes
•
Companies continue being exposed to driving under various weather conditions, exposing truckers to the dangers of hauling heavy loads of cargo under dangerous conditions.
•
Businesses are enhancing driver safety by investing in fleets and equipping vehicles with the latest technology. Modern trucks now feature cargo sensors and digital dashboards, which, according to Freightwaves, lead to a 26.0% drop in accidents and a 49.0% reduction in accident costs.
Electrification is supported by a heavier weight allowance
•
The adoption of electric vehicles continues to face many hurdles. The lack of charging infrastructure and the range are the most discussed topics in the industry. Another key point of concern remains around the weight of the batteries, which are much heavier than their diesel-powered engine counterparts.
•
According to a study by the University of California, long-haul electric trucks are expected to be an estimated 5,000 pounds heavier than their diesel-powered counterparts. Rising weight will limit the cargo trucks that can be transported before breaching the weight-limit regulations. Higher vehicle weight is translating to a lower cargo amount, bringing down the revenue generated per trip.
•
The Canadian weight limits are under provincial jurisdiction, which marks a B-trailer's allowable weight at 92,000 pounds, in contrast to the 80,000 pounds mandated by the US federal government.

Volatility Moderate
Economic activity impacts the volumes of distributed freight
•
Specialized trucking serves an array of industries across the economy, ranging from manufacturing, wholesale and retail to chemical processors and the agricultural and construction sectors. Volatility in downstream markets translates to contract fluctuations.
•
The niche specialization and diversified operations mitigate some of the volatility from economic cycles. Many industries are dependent on trucking services because they require equipment, which can include light trucks, heavy machinery, or bulk commodity shipments, that can only be facilitated by companies operating in specialized long-distance trucking.
Volatility in oil prices impacts the surcharges truckers impose on their contracts
•
Fuel price fluctuations have an inherently more pronounced impact on long-distance trucking operations than on local and regional services because of the length of haul. The longer the distance transported, the more fuel truckers consume, driving up operating costs.
•
Trucking companies mitigate rising fuel costs by imposing surcharges on their contract rates and passing a portion of the spike in prices downstream to their clients, boosting their revenue.

The easing of the policy rate will lower the borrowing costs and support a recovery in economic activity. The expected pickup in growth and manufacturing output will drive distribution services across specialized freight transport and support expansion after a muted performance between 2019 and 2024 as the industry struggled to shake off the impacts of the pandemic.

The expected CAGR of 1.9% in consumer spending through 2029 [IBISWorld] and the broader forecast acceleration in the GDP growth from 1.1% in 2024 to 1.6% in 2025 [RBC] will boost contracting services from long-distance specialized freight across all key markets.
•
As the broader economy continues to improve and consumer spending rises, both manufacturing and retail activity are expected to climb, increasing the need for product transportation. The value of trade into and out of Canada is projected to steadily increase as its major trading partners recover from past economic challenges. This will drive demand for tank refrigeration truckers to transport goods to seaports and across the US borders.
Technological advances will continue reshaping long-distance trucking
•
Trucking companies will keep investing in remote monitoring systems and technology to strengthen the industry's resilience. While current trucks have some assistance features like lane monitoring sensors, manufacturers invest heavily in developing advanced trucks that reach Level 4 and 5 autonomy, phasing out the need for the driver.
•
The gradual rollout of autonomous trucks will transform long-distance logistics across all trucking industries while lowering the limitations presented by the Hours of Service (HOS) and Electronic Logging Devices (ELDs), which mandate the amount of time the drivers can spend on the road.
•
A study conducted by PwC estimates that autonomous freight trucks will operate an estimated 78.0% of the time, in contrast to the current 29.0%. While freight trucking is easier to streamline because of the less hazardous nature of the cargo transported, specialized trucks will benefit from the rollout nonetheless, even if the actual drive time comes in below estimates.
Pro-immigration policies are set to alleviate pressure from high driver turnover
•
Long-distance specialized freight trucking faces high driver turnover and vacancies due to extended time away from home and job stress. The Canadian market grapples with similar structural issues as the US long-distance trucking industry where drivers opt out of participating in the trucking labor force despite obtaining commercial driver licenses.
•
Companies are expected to continue rolling out policies aimed at improving the work-life balance and raising the salaries for workers to address the ongoing issues with worker retention and incentivize more skilled drivers to stay in the industry.
•
The shift in regulation will have a pronounced impact on the trucking industry's driver shortage and ongoing vacancies. The government introduced the Express Entry initiative to expedite immigration, enabling migrant workers to arrive in Canada within six months of applying. This initiative aims to quickly bring in new truckers to replace retiring employees and those not keen on the trucker lifestyle.
Life Cycle Mature
Contribution to GDP
The industry underperformed the broader economic activity as it struggled to rebound from the pandemic-induced slowdown. As economic conditions stabilize and demand for specialized freight returns to pre-pandemic levels, the industry is forecast to expand in line with the rate of the broader GDP, indicative of its maturity.
Market Saturation
The Canadian specialized freight market doesn't experience the same oversaturation as the truckload and long-distance trucking in the US. The market is more commodity-driven than in the US, and niche specialization and relationship-driven arrangements have prevented the industry from a flood of new entrants.
Innovation
While innovations occur at the manufacturing level, truckers benefit from improved vehicle aerodynamics, fuel-efficient engines, sensors and advances made in autonomous technology.
Consolidation
The incumbent trucking enterprises often branch out and diversify their operations by acquiring smaller businesses in the industry. Still, the fragmentation prevents any acquisition from having a material impact on the concentration.

Products and Services How are the industry’s products and services performing?
Wholesalers and manufacturers boost demand for packed goods distribution
•
Though the industry's central focus is distributing specialized goods, companies ship large quantities of standard-packed goods without using climate-control systems that can't fit into standardized trailers. Furniture, parts and accessories, home supplies and other products that sometimes can't be distributed using standard dry van trailers are distributed using flatbed trailers.
•
Wholesalers and manufacturers are the core markets for specialized distribution. The CAGR of 1.1% in retail sales supported the expansion of the product segment as businesses required the transport of oversized cargo between ports and warehouses to end destinations.
Economic volatility causes volatility in the dry bulk transport
•
Dry bulk cargo typically consists of commodities transported without the cargo being packaged before shipment, like grains, coal, ore, wood chips, salt, soybeans and other cash crops and minerals. Dry bulk carriers are often used to transport materials needed for construction projects, which causes demand for this trucking service to be highly dependent on real estate and construction activity in Canada.
•
Companies transporting dry bulk tend to operate trucks with pneumatic trailers equipped with valves that allow for easy loading and unloading of the cargo between stops. The contamination risk between shipments mandates truckers to undergo thorough trailer cleaning between shipments and incentivizes companies to develop established brand names, as improper cleaning
procedures can render the entire load worthless.
•
Long-distance dry bulk transport competes with rail distribution, which often offers more favorable pricing, especially when moving large loads. Rising fuel prices prompted shipments to shift from trucking to rail whenever possible and pressured the segment as truckers struggled to offer a higher value proposition than rail services.
•
Dry bulk transport is stabilizing after exhibiting volatility driven by supply chain disruptions between 2020 and 2022 and the rising diesel costs in 2022 and 2023.
The industry is a necessity for transporting bulk liquids and gases
•
Tanker trucking is the industry's preferred method of transporting chemicals, pressurized and nonpressurized gases, fuels and other bulk liquid goods. This service segment is vulnerable to the production volume of chemical product manufacturers and the level of downstream demand for petroleum and chemicals from consumers and other manufacturers.
•
Bulk liquids and gases are usually distributed by pipelines and rail. Trucking struggles to compete in regions with developed pipeline infrastructure as they transport liquids and gases directly from terminals to refineries and to end markets. In places lacking the pipeline infrastructure, trucking must contend with the presence of rail distribution, which is often more cost-effective than trucking when moving large volumes.
•
Trucking companies benefit from flexible delivery services that are often faster and offer more predictable delivery schedules. Trucking fills in the gaps in the supply chain where building out the

infrastructure isn't cost-effective, which is the case with areas that have low bulk liquid and gas requirements and tend to operate outside of the main provinces.
•
The improved route optimization and rising fuel efficiency in vehicles are supporting truckers when delivering bulk liquids and gasses to remote areas and supporting the expansion of the market despite competition from pipeline and rail distribution.
Insulated and refrigerated trucks help preserve climate-controlled packed goods
•
The climate-controlled packed goods segment includes the transportation of cargo that requires climate-controlled environments at mild, chilled or frozen temperatures. Insulated and refrigerated trailer trucks are used to transport these goods from manufacturers to wholesalers and often from wholesalers to retail distribution centres.
•
Climate-controlled packed goods include meat, poultry, seafood, dairy products, eggs, processed foods, frozen meals, fruits and vegetables, medical supplies, certain cosmetics, films and other perishable products. Demand for this service segment is sensitive to consumer demand for perishable goods but generally remains stable despite broader economic conditions.
•
Companies catering to this segment operate reefer fleets and transport goods from ports and production facilities into the end markets and warehouses for further distribution.
Stabilizing distribution patterns pressure the automobiles and light-duty truck transport
•
Companies offer automobile and light-duty truck hauling services using flatbed trailers. While rail is the preferred distribution method, trucking often offers a higher degree of flexibility as distribution isn't tied to a fixed rail infrastructure.
•
Transport of automobiles via trucks remains above the 2019 levels but continues to taper off since peaking in 2021. The distribution of cars by trucks isn't cost-efficient as trucks are limited by the size of their trailers and lack the economies of scale required to make long-term contracts worthwhile for the vendors.
•
The initial spike in distribution was caused by supply chain disruptions and the chip shortage, which forced manufacturers to seek alternative distribution routes, often in remote areas, without access to rail infrastructure capable of facilitating the transport, which drove the utilization of trucking and driving up the market share of the product segment.
•
The automobile and light-duty trucks segment is expected to continue moderating amid normalizing distribution patterns. Given the last-mile delivery requirements, the trend for automobile transport is expected to be particularly pronounced in the long-distance distribution and less so in the local distribution.
Expansion of core services causes the other segment to stagnate
•
Other transportation includes live animals and transport of miscellaneous goods that include hazardous and non-hazardous waste. Companies transporting live animals typically do so by acquiring gooseneck trailers, also known as bull haulers. The trucks are designed with special gates and provide adequate airflow for the animals.
•
A growing regulatory oversight over conditions of livestock transport is limiting the growth of the segment as regulation prohibits transporting livestock for more than 36 hours without food, water, or rest. Compromised cattle can only be transported for a maximum of 12 hours. The regulations and oversight over the conditions of the animals present many barriers for truckers looking to enter the market and keep growth within the segment relatively stagnant.
•
Waste management fits into the industry's services but doesn't have a material impact on the revenue generated. Waste typically doesn't have to be transported over long distances and is usually handled by the local services or dump trucks at the site.

What impacts the industry’s market share concentration?
New entrants are specializing and keeping the concentration low
•
Vendors often outsource distribution contracts through freight brokerages or directly contract trucking businesses, which then occasionally pass on the contracts to for-hire truckers or complete the order through internal fleets. Many companies operating in the industry are relatively small, with an estimated 96.4% of companies employing less than 50 employees.
•
Truckers fulfilling long-distance services benefit from relatively low barriers to entry. Companies don't require extensive infrastructure to fulfill order deliveries, as most warehousing requirements fall on the vendor requesting the distribution services. This is in contrast to less-than-truckload distribution, where oftentimes the warehousing and terminal infrastructure fall on the distributor.
•
Small and new entrants are developing niche expertise in transporting commodities, particularly in oil, chemical and dry bulk shipments, which require tank carriers or pneumatic trailers to fulfill orders. Many of the businesses enter the industry through being licensed in a particular distribution type and then branch out once they establish a presence in their core market.
Fragmentation is muting the impact of merger and acquisition activity
•
The cost of driving a truck loaded with freight to a destination doesn't change with the size of the company's operations, meaning the benefits from economies of scale are muted. While large companies gain competitive advantages from maintaining expansive operations, including forward fuel purchasing contracts with wholesalers and retaining repair personnel, the lack of economies of scale contributed to no major companies in the industry.
•
The existing fragmentation of the industry also discourages mergers and buyouts from large truckers as it reduces the amount of market share to be gained through one acquisition.
•
The climbing truck costs and growing adoption of vehicles are pushing up costs and raising barriers to entry. The gradual rise in barriers to entry is pressuring new establishments and elevating the advantage of incumbent enterprises when competing with small businesses, particularly in relation to securing contracts from large vendors.

Labour Expenses
•

Regulation & Policy Moderate Increasing
What regulations impact the industry?
Transport Canada safety initiatives
Transport Canada's Motor Carrier Division is in charge of safety initiatives that look to reduce the number of fatalities, injuries and crashes associated with large commercial trucks. The Division issues regulations surrounding certification and hours of service, limiting driving time to 13 hours a day for each driver. Transport Canada also places a cap of 70 hours on how long a single truck can be driven in a consecutive week.
Provincial trucking regulations
Through the Motor Vehicle Transport Act, the federal government gives provinces and territories the ability to create their own highway safety initiatives. Matters concerning truck weight and dimensions are typically left to provincial governments, and provinces usually handle the registration of commercial vehicles.
Federal emissions restrictions
The emissions of specialized trucks are regulated by the Canadian Environmental Protection Act, and standards are closely harmonized with those of the US's Environmental Protection Agency. Both Canadian and US government agencies have both imposed federal restrictions on lead-based gasoline, offered subsidies to gasoline companies that use ethanol-based fuel and set standards for nitrogen oxide and sulphur emissions. The Canadian Trucking Alliance has also added their own guidelines that apply to the many trucks in their fleet.

Surcharges allow nonemployers and trucking companies to pass rising fuel costs downstream to their clients. The specialized nature supports their negotiating power. Still, the industry's competitive nature prevents businesses from fully passing costs downstream and forces them to absorb parts of the cost. Rising fuel prices

The moderating growth in new and used vehicle prices will prompt a decline in insurance and maintenance costs relative to revenue expansion, supporting a profit recovery. A slowing consumer-price index and a rebounding economic activity energized by monetary easing will support freight volumes and transport of petroleum and reefer contracts, spurring revenue expansion and allowing profit to recover as conditions stabilize.

Key Success Factors
How do successful businesses overcome volatility? Adapt quickly to changing regulations
Staying agile and compliant with evolving transportation laws helps trucking companies avoid penalties and operational disruptions, ensuring smooth and continuous services even in a regulatory landscape that's prone to change.
Establish an efficient funding structure and efficient, cost-effective distribution channels
Cultivating robust partnerships with financial institutions provides trucking companies with the financial flexibility needed to navigate economic downturns and invest in necessary fleet upgrades or expansions regardless of market conditions.
What products or services do successful businesses offer? Ensure prompt delivery to market
Rapid and reliable delivery is crucial in long-distance freight trucking. Ensuring goods arrive on time builds trust with clients, enhances customer satisfaction and provides a competitive edge in the logistics industry.
Provide superior after-sales service
Offering excellent customer support after deliveries can strengthen client relationships and encourage repeat business. Superior after-sales service, like real-time tracking updates and quick issue resolution, enhances customer loyalty and elevates company reputation in a competitive market.
How do businesses use location to their advantage? Successful industrial relations policy
The industry is heavily unionized, meaning successful negotiation with unions is key to retaining staff and competing with nonunion companies.
Access to multiskilled and flexible workforce
Since truck drivers endure long working hours, uncomfortable working conditions and extended time away from home, it is important that companies offer sufficient wages and benefits to retain a strong and reliable workforce.
How do successful businesses handle concentration? Maintain efficient work practices
Streamlining operations and maintaining efficiency lowers costs and enhances reliability, which is crucial in the competitive tank and refrigeration trucking sector, where timely deliveries and cost-effectiveness are key to satisfying clients and maintaining market position.
Quickly adopt new technology
Rapidly integrating new technology enhances operation efficiency and reduces downtime. Staying technologically advanced ensures optimal performance, meets regulatory standards and aligns with evolving customer expectations for quality and reliability in a competitive market.
How can potential entrants overcome barriers to entry? Comply with government regulations
Adhering to stringent Canadian transportation regulations is crucial for operating smoothly in the long-distance trucking sector. This not only avoids legal pitfalls but also enhances credibility, reducing barriers for consistent operations amidst strict compliance landscapes.
Develop strong working relationships with lenders
Building solid relationships with lenders can provide the necessary capital for fleet expansion and operational improvements. The financial backing is essential for overcoming initial entry barriers and maintaining a competitive edge in the specialized freight trucking market.
How do successful businesses compete with substitutes? Develop strong technical product knowledge

Having deep technical expertise ensures companies can effectively manage and troubleshoot their specialized equipment. This capability enhances reliability and service excellence, making their offerings more attractive compared to substitute transportation options.
Establish brand names
Building a recognizable and trusted brand fosters client confidence and loyalty. By establishing a strong brand presence, Businesses can reduce the likelihood of customers switching to substitutes, reinforcing their position in Canada's competitive trucking landscape.
How do successful businesses manage buyer & supplier power? Offer a competitively priced product
Maintaining competitive pricing allows the company to appeal to a broader range of clients, reducing buyer power. This strategy encourages customer loyalty and makes it harder for buyers to shift to competitors simply for cost reasons.
Have a large supply contract
Holding substantial supply contracts can strengthen the company's position in negotiating with buyers and suppliers. It ensures continuous demand for services, enhances revenue stability and can provide a buffer against price fluctuations.

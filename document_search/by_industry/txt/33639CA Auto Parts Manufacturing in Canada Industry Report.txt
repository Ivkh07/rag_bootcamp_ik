Executive Summary Revving up: Revenue growth is expected to be aided by a rise in new vehicle sales
Auto parts manufacturers build miscellaneous parts and equipment, including exhaust systems, airbags, heating, ventilation, air conditioning systems (HVAC) and filtration devices. Manufacturers faced significant challenges during the COVID-19 pandemic, with social distancing significantly reducing travel and weakening the manufacturing sector, causing revenue to contract. However, mass exoduses back to work and greater economic stability have encouraged consumers to drive more, creating greater demand for new and used cars and repairs and indirectly boosting demand for auto parts manufacturers. Even so, climbing interest rates have threatened the entire automobile manufacturing supply chain, with higher borrowing and auto loan costs stifling new car sales. Overall, revenue has climbed at an expected CAGR of 1.2% to $7.9 billion through the current period, including a 1.9% jump in 2024, where profit reached 8.7%.
Canadian auto parts manufacturers endured significant supply chain volatility following the pandemic and the Russian invasion of Ukraine. Higher input costs limited profit growth in 2022 and 2023, especially after skyrocketing steel and semiconductor prices. Offshoring trends have also battered auto parts manufacturers; low wages in Mexico and China have encouraged massive offshoring. Similarly, competition from countries with advanced technology, manufacturing and automotive sectors has overwhelmed Canadian producers, leading to significant import penetration and market saturation.
Canadian auto parts manufacturers will benefit from strong economic conditions through the outlook period. Stable economic growth and muted inflation will support consumer confidence and real incomes, boosting car sales. Average vehicle age will also increase, supporting aftermarkets, specifically demand for repairs on existing vehicles. The Canadian dollar will also depreciate, boosting exports and partially counteracting offshoring trends from the past decade. Electric and autonomous vehicles, alongside other safety and sustainability innovations, will also create more niche markets for auto parts manufacturers. Overall, revenue for auto parts manufacturers will rebound at an expected CAGR of 1.4% to $8.5 billion through the outlook period, where profit will reach 8.7%.

What's driving current industry performance?
Climbing interest rates threaten auto parts manufacturers
•
The Bank of Canada has raised interest rates significantly through 2022 and 2023 to combat stifling inflation across the Canadian economy. The BOC has held rates steady at 5.0% through the first half of 2024 before beginning cuts in the latter half of the year. Borrowing costs have skyrocketed, decimating consumer confidence, especially among lower-income consumers.
•
Most households and individuals also take out auto loans for new cars. Climbing rates have made loans unsavory for most and entirely unattainable for some. Many consumers have instead chosen to ride out their current vehicles, paying for less costly repairs.
•
As a result, aftermarkets, like repair shops, have represented an increasing revenue source through the current period. Overall, aftermarkets generate lower per-unit revenue than automobile manufacturers; this trend has threatened auto parts manufacturers.
Auto parts manufacturers have increasingly moved production offshore
•
Like downstream automakers, auto parts manufacturers have taken advantage of more lax environmental and labour regulations abroad to cut production costs. Many parts producers have moved production to Mexico and China to remain near key buyers.
•
This offshoring trend has led to a decade-long rise in import penetration alongside a stagnating business entry rate. Many domestic companies have struggled to match the prices offered by low-cost importers, forcing businesses to exit or consolidate. Offshoring can also lead to subdued innovation in Canada.
•
Many manufacturers have targeted smaller companies with lucrative contracts and strong R&D to secure revenue streams. Similarly, many manufacturers have focused on higher-margin products, like specialty auto parts, emphasizing quality, sustainability and consistency to appeal to automakers, especially luxury brands.
•
Many auto parts manufacturers will also offer after-sales services and design support to make their products more appealing to automakers than competitors and imports.
Pent-up travel demand counteracts economic headwinds
•
As the federal government and provincial authorities lift pandemic-era travel and work restrictions, people have increasingly traveled to vacation spots and work, necessitating new cars and repairs. In particular, rental car services increased demand for new cars, rippling through the automotive supply chain.
•
However, the semiconductor chip shortage has been a crucial limiting factor in new car production. The shortage has made it difficult for

•

Auto parts manufacturers have ridden long-term contracts and elevated aftermarket demand to sustain revenue despite high interest rates and volatile consumer confidence.
Skyrocketing input prices add to volatility
•
Auto parts manufacturers grappled with major supply chain issues following the pandemic. In particular, steel prices ballooned, creating supply backlogs and forcing manufacturers to push costs onto automakers. In turn, automakers pushed prices onto consumers.
•
Even so, each wrung of the supply chain absorbed some costs, leading to weak profit and significant uncertainty. The Russian invasion of Ukraine added to supply chain uncertainty, especially in ferrous metal markets. As a major crude oil supplier, global sanctions on Russia have led to higher prices for oil byproducts, like rubber, steel and adhesives.
•
Companies have started to benefit from greater stability as input prices recede to previous levels at the end of the current period. Even so, companies must lower prices to accommodate for sinking input prices, leading to temporary revenue dips.

Supply chain disruptions add to volatility
•
Auto parts manufacturers rely on raw inputs, like steel and aluminum, alongside semi-finished goods, like semiconductors, filters and nylons, to create parts for automakers and aftermarkets.
•
Supply chain disruptions during the pandemic led to significant price shocks. Since auto parts are essential for automobile manufacturers, many manufacturers have increased prices. Automakers then raised car prices, passing costs onto consumers.
•
In general, high input prices have threatened purchasing costs, leading to subdued profit. Conversely, companies will need to slash prices to remain competitive as input prices ease through the outlook period.
Consumer behavior impacts the industry's performance
•
Consumer confidence and spending determine how many cars consumers purchase in a given year, determining sales for automakers. Lower consumer confidence constrains demand from automakers but supports demand from aftermarkets, specifically repair shops. Even so, aftermarket demand is minuscule compared with automakers; poor consumer confidence is a net-negative for auto parts manufacturers.
•
Climbing prime rates and falling interest rates pose major threats to volatility. Auto loans become more expensive and consumers grow unwilling to make major purchases. Even so, pent-up demand for travel and a return to work has encouraged many consumers to purchase new cars regardless.
Union pressures and technological innovation add to uncertainty
•
Unifor labour strikes through the current period have created volatility. Strikes can halt production lines and cause significant delays. Strikes also influence wages, often contributing to lower returns and uncertainty.
•
Similarly, the shift towards electric vehicles (EVs) requires different parts than traditional gasoline cars. Companies that don't keep up may see their revenues decline. New investment into EV and self-driving technology may present lucrative opportunities for parts manufacturers but the sheer uncertainty of these markets adds to revenue volatility.

Outlook  2024-29 Revenue CAGR +1.4%
Rebouding economic conditions will create revenue growth
•
Despite severe economic challenges through the current period, including COVID-19, rampant inflation, climbing interest rates and recessionary fears, companies will benefit from rebounding economic conditions and rosier consumer confidence through the outlook period.
•
In particular, stable growth and falling inflation will raise real incomes and consumer confidence, spurring car usage and new vehicle purchases. Similarly, the Bank of Canada will continue June 2024 rate cuts through the outlook period. These trends will spur demand from manufacturers and aftermarkets.
•
In general, strengthening economic conditions will inject much-needed stability into the automobile manufacturing supply chain. This stability will spur downstream consumer demand, giving auto parts producers more opportunities to secure original equipment manufacturing contracts from automakers.
Innovation will create new opportunites for auto parts manufacturers
•
Automakers have added many advanced features to vehicles, including filtration systems to limit pathogens and allergens alongside new airbags that cushion the head better on impact. Numerous recalls for airbags over the past decade have spurred the latter’s innovations. These innovations have generally created new growth opportunities for auto parts manufacturers, which will persist through the outlook period.
•
The increasing prominence of electric and autonomous vehicles will also create demand for a host of niche, highly advanced auto parts. This trend will eventually lead to further specialization and fragmentation among parts manufacturers.
•
Even so, electric vehicles lack exhaust systems. As EVs grow more popular, demand for the exhaust segment will falter, posing a threat to specialized manufacturers. Overall, auto parts manufacturers will need to adapt to major demand and technological shifts through the outlook period to remain successful.
Consumers will replace aging vehicles
•
Households and individuals regularly replace aging vehicles with newer, more sustainable, efficient cars. Consumers often use improved mileage as a benchmark for trading in or replacing current vehicles, leading to more car sales. Rising new car sales will support auto parts manufacturers through the outlook period.
•
In particular, more efficient electric vehicles will create replacement demand. Even so, this trend relies on continued government support and strong public spending for charging stations and related EV infrastructure. Slowing EV adoption rates may give auto parts manufacturers more time to adapt to these changes and leverage robust growth in hybrid markets.
•
Existing cars have also lasted longer than previous iterations, decreasing replacement rates so demand for aftermarket parts will strengthen as the existing vehicle fleet ages. In particular, the Canadian Vehicle Survey (CVS) has stated that the average vehicle age in Canada is eleven years.
•
New and used car prices will also continue to climb steadily, further encouraging consumers to repair, not replace. Do-it-yourself (DIY) car repair will also remain a popular fringe trend, supporting demand for car and truck parts from consumers.
Foreign products will dominate Canadian markets
•
Canadian auto parts manufacturers have historically faced intense pressure from imported products. In particular, Mexico and China represent major outsourcing threats, given lax labour and environmental laws. Many supply chain members will continue to move production

Products and Services How are the industry’s products and services performing?
Electric vehicle production threatens exhaust systems
•
Auto parts manufacturers sell exhaust systems to automakers and aftermarket buyers. Automakers use exhaust systems to vent toxic byproducts from engine combustion to keep passengers safe.
•
Stricter regulation standards, particularly regarding emissions (carbon monoxide and dioxide alongside nitrogen oxide), have also forced automakers to alter exhaust system designs. New designs may drive revenue.
•
In comparison. electric vehicles don't require exhaust systems since they don't create any emissions or exhaust gases. Federal programs have encouraged companies to prioritize electrical vehicle production, posing a major threat to the exhaust system segment. Even so, lukewarm EV adoption may give parts manufacturers more time to adapt to changes.
Fuel tanks and filters are essential in combustion vehicles
•
Buyers use filters in internal combustion engines to keep the air and cabin in a car clean and ensure the fuel systems aren't contaminated. Filters are necessary for all combustion vehicles, leading to stable demand.
•
Even electric vehicles use filters extensively, particularly to maintain proper cabin airflow. However, EVs don't have internal combustion engines, constraining demand for filters as more consumers shift to electric vehicles.
Companies are developing more advanced air conditioning and HVAC systems
•
HVAC systems include air conditioning, heating and ventilation systems in cabin cars. Many automakers have competed to create better HVAC systems to attract more consumers. In particular, luxury automakers have advertised superior cabin temperature systems, leading to elevated demand.
•
Companies have targeted HVAC systems to create more fuel-efficient cars. More effective air conditioners use less power, enabling cars to run for longer while using less power. HVAC innovation can help automakers hit fuel efficiency goals.
•
Automakers have also demanded HVAC systems that are better at filtering out air contaminants, pathogens and smells, especially after the pandemic. These demands have precipitated innovation and demand for more expensive HVAC systems.
Airbags are essential in all vehicles
•
Automakers use airbags to protect a vehicle's occupant from a potential collision. The price of many key airbag components, like nylon, don't face drastic fluctuations, so spending on these materials depends on car and truck usage.
•
Airbags are also highly nondiscretionary, creating steady demand. Faulty airbags can severely damage a manufacturer's reputation, creating an extreme emphasis on quality and safety. Federal organizations extensively test airbags.
•
Innovation has also driven demand for new airbags. Modern crash-detection sensors come equipped with a network of instruments, including accelerometers, gyroscopes and brake-pressure sensors for more precise and effective response during an accident. These systems create a faster airbag response, protecting drivers and passengers.
Other products cover a wide array of essential car parts
•
Products included in the "other" category consist of wheel frames, motor vehicle axles, motor vehicle convertible tops, windshield washer fluid, shock absorbers, steering wheels, electrical components and similar products.
•
Companies often specialize in certain products within the other category, leading to significant fragmentation and preventing one company from dominating the market. These aforementioned products are also integral in vehicle production, preventing severe downturns.
•
For example, auto parts manufacturers have shifted toward lightweight alloy wheel frames as automakers strive to boost efficiency and enhance aesthetic appeal. These specialized wheels also cater to the demand for aftermarket customizations.

What are innovations in industry products and services? Moderate
The pandemic encouraged companies to create new filtration systems
•
The pandemic exacerbated fears about the spread of pathogens through the air, creating elevated demand for vehicles with advanced filtration and HVAC systems to significantly reduce the spread of germs.
•
For example, the Swedish car manufacturer Volvo has added filters that use ionization and synthetic filters that remove 95.0% of impurities from the outside air. Tesla adopted a similar model in 2016, where it can remove the outside air of pollen, bacteria and pollution before it enters the cabin.
Newly released airbags enhance safety features
•
Parent company, Honda, recently introduced the Acura TLX with a new airbag that catches the head in a pocket, increasing the likelihood that the person doesn't slam themselves onto a hard surface.
•
The push to reduce harm and mortality from car accidents is a major reason for the creation of these airbag systems. Increasing highway safety regulations will push manufacturers to innovate newer, safer products.

Major Markets What’s influencing demand from the industry’s markets?
Auto parts manufacturers rely on demand from automakers
•
Automakers generate the majority of revenue for auto parts manufacturers with long-term supply contracts. Most automakers have contracts with multiple parts producers for different pieces of equipment. Many automakers, like Hyundai and Toyota, have also established internal parts manufacturing divisions or source from manufacturers in their home countries, diminishing the industry's growth potential.
•
Auto parts manufacturers have increasingly sold directly to automakers, cutting wholesale and distribution costs. Parts producers have integrated online ordering systems, enabling automakers and parts manufacturers to interface directly.
Consumers rely on aftermarkets during poor economic conditions
•
Auto parts manufacturers sell products like wheel frames and aesthetic parts to retailers, repair shops, wholesalers, businesses and consumers for resale and to repair used cars. These products commonly lack the original equipment manufacturer branding but often have the same, if not identical, quality.
•
Since used vehicles are less expensive, many households will choose to buy or make repairs on old cars when consumer confidence and discretionary income remain depressed.
•
According to the Canadian Vehicle Study, the average age of cars on the road in Canada is more than 11 years old. An aging consumer vehicle fleet suggest greater demand for replacement parts and repairs, increasing demand for aftermarkets.
Wholesalers and distributors play a key role
•
Many auto parts manufacturers rely on wholesalers and distributors to store and ship orders between original equipment
manufacturers and automakers, acting as intermediaries and reaping surcharges.
•
These intermediaries remove certain boundaries for auto parts manufacturers. Companies don't have to invest in internal warehouses and distribution teams, enabling them to focus on production efficiency, technological integration and innovation instead.

Canadian auto parts manufacturers often prioritize producing within specific niches and verticals, as increased specialization often enables companies to maximize profit. Regulation & Policy Moderate Steady
What regulations impact the industry?
Vehicle emission standards
Canadian auto parts manufacturers must adhere to emission standards, covering exhaust systems and catalytic converters. The Canadian government regularly revisits and updates these standards, often creating more strict emission standards for vehicles.
Innovation mandate
Companies are encouraged to innovate to remain competitive, resulting from strict emission standards. Canada unveiled the Electric Vehicle Availability Standard, requiring all new cars, SUVs, crossovers and light-duty pickup trucks sold in Canada to be 100% zero-emission by 2035.
Highway safety regulations
Highway safety and other commissions regulate automobile parts produced by Canadian manufacturers. This oversight ensures their safety and quality. In particular, Canada's Motor Vehicle Safety Act (MVSA) requires manufacturers to comply with stringent performance requirements to protect public safety. Non-compliance can lead to recalls and heavy fines.
Canadian Environmental Protection Act (CEPA)
CEPA governs the environmental impact of manufacturing processes. This regulation requires automobile parts producers to manage and reduce pollutants, emissions and waste. Additionally, it promotes the use of sustainable materials and recycling, helping minimize the automotive industry's environmental footprint.
Workplace standards
These regulations, enunciated under the Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety (CCOHS), ensure a safe and healthy work environment for employees. They require manufacturers to implement practices that mitigate risks related to machinery, chemical handling, and ergonomics. Regular inspections and employee training are essential to compliance, fostering a safer workplace for everyone involved.

What assistance is available to this industry?
Government Electric vehicle funding
Federal and provincial governments have set up various funds, including the Strategic Innovation Fund (SIF). These funds will support automotive innovation. This support focuses on companies in the hybrid and electric vehicle supply chains. Otherwise, auto parts manufacturers receive little direct government support.
Government Automotive Supplier Competitiveness Improvement Program (ASCIP)
In Ontario, ASCIP provides financial support for capital equipment and technology. This program helps Ontario-based auto parts manufacturers enhance their productivity and innovation. Companies can improve their efficiency and global competitiveness by investing in advanced machinery and technological upgrades.
Non-government Automotive Parts Manufacturers' Association (APMA)
The APMA, or Automotive Parts Manufacturers' Association, is a Canadian national association that denotes manufacturers actively involved in various stages like designing, producing and distributing automotive parts. The association advocates for members and offers networking opportunities.
Non-government Canadian Association of Moldmakers (CAMM)
CAMM is an association that represents companies engaged in the creation and manufacturing of molds, tools, dies and other elements used in the auto sector. Services include providing networking opportunities, training programs and advocating for their members' interests.
Non-government Original Equipment Suppliers Association (OESA) of Canada
OESA offers members data and insights about Canada's auto parts manufacturers. OESA is one of the four main divisions of the Motor & Equipment Manufacturer’s Association (MEMA). OESA has its headquarters in the United States but also operates in Canada.
Non-government Canadian Automotive Service Information Standard (CASIS)
CASIS ensures that independent auto parts manufacturers and service providers have access to necessary vehicle service information. This information-sharing agreement helps manufacturers produce compatible parts and offers service providers the data needed for effective repairs, leveling the playing field and promoting industry-wide collaboration.

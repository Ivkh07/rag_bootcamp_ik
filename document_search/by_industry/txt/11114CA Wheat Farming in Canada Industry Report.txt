Executive Summary Weak harvest: Declining wheat prices and stagnate demand will result in plummeting revenue
Canadian wheat farms have grown despite considerable economic volatility through the end of 2024. The industry is highly globalized and reliant on trade, which has improved following the dismantling of the Canadian Wheat Board in 2012; the board had restricted the marketing freedom of farms. Demand for Canadian wheat abroad has risen because of its reputation for high quality. Favourable price increases driven by world wheat prices have improved farm revenue and profitability. Improved trade ties with emerging economies have also aided growth by helping farmers tap into demand from new markets. Wheat farming revenue is expected to climb at a CAGR of 4.7% to an estimated $16.2 billion through the end of 2024, despite a decrease of 2.5% in 2024 because of waning wheat demand.
Strong wheat prices have been the primary driver of revenue growth in recent years. The world price of wheat grew by a staggering 43.3% in 2021, followed by 35.5% in 2022, as global supply levels contracted. Canadian wheat prices surged the same year amid an intense drought that cut crop yields, making wheat a rare commodity. Strong revenue growth, combined with technological advancements, has enabled profit expansion for Canada's wheat farmers.
Moving forward, a continued gain in the world price of wheat, counteracted some by flagging demand, will drag down revenue growth. Exports will continue to expand, albeit at a slower pace, as external competition from other grains and wheat production in other countries intensifies. Uncertainty regarding global wheat yields will also pose a threat to Canadian wheat farmers. Wheat is susceptible to various weather changes, meaning that industry performance can be drastically disrupted at any time. Revenue is expected to climb at a CAGR of 0.8% to $16.8 billion through the end of 2029.

What's driving current industry performance?
A growing price of wheat has driven industry growth
•
Industry performance is closely tied to the world price of wheat. Farmers can charge a premium for their harvest when wheat prices are high. The world price of wheat has grown at a CAGR of 12.3% through the end of 2024, spiking in double digits in 2020, 2021 and 2022.
•
Supply largely determines the price of wheat. Prices surge when severe weather or insect infestations cut crop yields. A severe drought in 2020 and 2021 made wheat more scarce and boosted prices.
•
Revenue reached a peak in 2021 and declined in 2022, but has remained around 2021 levels in 2023 and 2024. This has stemmed from year-to-year volatility in price, as it fell over 2023 in reaction to years of increases.
Growth in exports has fuelled revenue expansion
•
Exports account for more than half of wheat farming revenue. The value of exports jumped in 2022 and 2023 because of lower global supply and strong growth in the world price of wheat.
•
Exports of wheat to all of Canada's major trading partners have expanded in recent years. China has been the largest source of growth for wheat farmers, with exports to China growing more than quintupling between 2017 and 2023, with more than 20.0% growth in 2024.
•
Export volumes have been bolstered by the strong reputation of Canadian wheat products in foreign markets. Canadian wheat has been praised for its versatility, quality, milling characteristics and superior end-product performance.
Spring wheat remains the dominant product
•
Spring wheat, which is typically planted in the spring and harvested in the summer, is the most popular industry product by far. Domestic spring wheat is high quality and is used for a variety of baked goods, including pastries, cakes, cereal, crackers, biscuits and pasta. Spring wheat accountgs for nearly three-quarters of industry production.
•
Production of durum wheat, which is higher in protein and contains more vitamins and minerals, has become more popular, though. Still, spring wheat accounts for the vast majority of the industry's wheat production. Spring wheat is favoured by farmers because it grows much better in Canada's climate.
Profitability has improved alongside growing wheat prices
•
High global wheat prices have allowed farmers to raise their prices and generate additional revenue, which has made fixed costs more

manageable. Combined with capital investments that have reduced farmers' reliance on labour, profit has expanded for Canadian wheat farmers.
•
Volatile fertilizer prices have limited profit growth, though. The price of fertilizer doubled between 2019 and 2022, boosting input costs for farmers since fertilizer is a widely used component in agriculture. In the years since, this has come down somewhat.
•
Growth in purchase costs has been a major concern for wheat farmers in recent years. Costs for other major inputs, like seeds and supplies, have grown in tandem with fertilizer prices.
Volatility High
Wheat prices are determined globally
•
Global supply and demand conditions dictate the price of wheat. The global supply of wheat is the primary determinant of price, since demand tends to be relatively steady.
•
Changes in the world price of wheat have fuelled revenue volatility in recent years. Strong growth in wheat prices has been the primary contributor to revenue growth.
Weather affects the supply of wheat
•
Weather conditions are important for any agricultural industry. Adverse weather can negatively affect yields and crop quality. Genetically modified crops have improved the viability of some crops during instances of adverse weather, but many consumers remain hesitant about consuming these products.
•
In 2022, droughts in parts of the United States contributed to a global supply shortage of wheat. This, combined with growing difficulties in Ukraine due to the war, caused the world price of wheat to expand 35.5% in 2022 alone.

How do successful businesses overcome volatility?
Ability to alter goods and services produced in favour of market conditions
Farmers with the ability to diversify their output between wheat and other types of crops will be more profitable and better able to withstand periods of poor market conditions for any single type of product. This would help combat revenue volatility.
Specialize in a product that is considered essential by legislators
As an integral part of the domestic food supply, wheat farming is considered essential by legislators. This has enabled farmers to continue operations largely uninhibited, which reduces disruptions in operations and cuts down on volatility.
Outlook  2024-29 Revenue CAGR +0.8%
Higher wheat prices will provide a growth opportunity
•
Revenue growth is forecast to be small in the coming years. This is primarily supported by rising wheat prices, which are expected to be more than double 2019 levels by 2029.
•
Wheat farmers depend on global wheat price trends. When the price falls, it prevents farmers from charging a premium for their wheat and forces them to drop prices to remain competitive, since price is the primary form of competition. Higher prices enable farmers to charge more, boosting revenue.
•
Despite an increase in prices, profit is forecast to inch downward in the coming years. Capital intensity for wheat farmers is extremely high and they will need to expand on this to remain competitive and efficient. This will place downward pressure on profit.
Human consumption trends will cause headwinds
•
Many consumers have emphasized a low-carb diet to improve blood sugar and cholesterol levels, narrowing the market for wheat and contributing to falling revenue. Still, wheat products comprise a significant portion of the Canadian diet, which will continue moving forward.
•
Per capita wheat consumption is forecast to shrink over the coming years, mirroring a long-term trend over the past decade. In addition to low-carb diets, other grain alternatives, including quinoa and others, are more popular and will continue to surge.
•
Certain diet plans, including the Mediterranean diet, don't stress a reduced carb intake. The growing popularity of this diet will benefit Canadian wheat farmers. Wheat will remain a staple in the diets of most Canadians, preventing siginficant declines in revenue.
Depreciation is forecast to aid export expansion
•
Export markets account for the largest share of sales of Canadian wheat. When there is less demand from export markets, it hinders revenue growth, though revenue often expands when exports are on the upswing.
•
The Canadian Effective Exchange Rate Index (CEER), which measures the value of the Canadian dollar against its trading partners, is expected to reach a peak in 2024 and begin depreciating to close to pre-pandemic levels. This makes exports more popular in the global marketplace.
•
The most popular export destinations for Canadian wheat are China, the United States, Indonesia and Japan. The proximity of domestic wheat, most of which is produced west of Saskatchewan, to Asian markets incentivizes trade for many Canadian farmers.

Market Saturation
The industry is made up of small, mostly independent farmers and no single farm dominates the market. The lack of product differentiation and the relatively low barriers to entry have prevented market saturation from occurring.

Innovation
There is minimal innovation among wheat farmers. Most innovation occurs in upstream manufacturing industries, which are constantly developing new equipment and technology for farmers to purchase.

Products and Services How are the industry’s products and services performing?
Spring wheat production is highly dominant
•
Most domestic production is spring wheat, which is typically planted in the spring and harvested during the summer. There are several different varieties of spring wheat, including extra strong, hard red and prairie spring, each of which varies slightly in terms of their overall end uses.
•
Domestic spring wheat products are the highest quality and are used for a variety of baked goods, including pastries, cakes, cereal, crackers, biscuits and pasta.
•
Production of spring wheat has inched downward in recent years in favour of other wheat types, despite its relative dominance. According to 2024 figures from Statistics Canada, the volume of spring wheat produced domestically has fallen slightly between 2019 and 2024.
Winter wheat production comprises a modest share of revenue
•
Winter wheat is planted in the autumn and harvested in the summer or early autumn of the following year. Similar to spring wheat, there are various strains of winter wheat, including Canada eastern hard red, eastern hard white winter and eastern soft red winter.
•
Winter wheat is also used for a variety of different products, including bread, cakes, pastries, cereal, crackers, biscuits and noodles. According to Statistics Canada, production volumes of winter wheat have expanded significantly since 2019, nearly doubling.
•
Notably, winter wheat typically has higher average yields than other types of wheat. According to the Western Winter Wheat Initiative, the wintering dormancy stage helps to protect the crop from a variety of diseases, pests and other difficulties.
Durum wheat production increases
•
Durum wheat is produced in the spring and it is primarily used to make pasta because of its high gluten content and colour.
Domestic production of durum wheat has been rising since 2019.
•
Durum wheat is more drought resistant than other forms of wheat and can more readily be used in semi-arid regions. Domestic durum wheat is of particularly high quality and is a common export.
•
Durum wheat has recently been at the centre of trade tensions with Italy. Italy had previously been a key destination for Canadian durum exports, amounting to about one-quarter of the total. Italy has since instituted country-of-origin labelling requirements on pasta and since that time, durum exports to Italy have been significantly cut.

What are innovations in industry products and services? Low
Genetically modified seeds could become available
•
Genetically modified crops like corn, soybeans and cotton have become commercially available in recent decades. Genetic modifications have helped to enhance productivity while improving resistance to certain pests and diseases.
•
There are no commercially produced genetically modified strains of wheat. Farmers have been reluctant to use genetically modified seeds for wheat since it is directly consumed by people, unlike soybeans and most corn products.
There is significant upstream innovation
•
Changes to farming equipment, fertilizers and other chemicals are the largest source of innovation for farmers. Most of these innovations promise to increase yields, improve quality and save farmers time.
•
If a wheat farmer does not have adequate equipment and materials, it will be difficult for them to effectively compete. Capital costs have steadily increased for farmers in recent years alongside growing input costs.
 Key Success Factors
What products or services do successful businesses offer?
Alter goods and services produced in favor of market conditions
The ability to alter the balance between wheat and other crops or livestock production is a key factor for a farm's viability. Diversified farms are more profitable and are able to withstand downturns in the profitability of wheat.
Establish supply contracts for key inputs
Farmers endure significant volatility with input prices. As consolidation has become more common among upstream suppliers, many farmers have established supply contracts to avoid being price gouged because of volatility.
Major Markets What’s influencing demand from the industry’s markets?
Animal feed revenue has had a downturn amid high prices
•
Most domestic demand for wheat comes from animal feed manufacturers. Animal feed has become more expensive in recent years, but at a slower rate than wheat. Switching grains is easier for animal feed than human food, causing this segment to shrink as a share of revenue.
•
Demand for wheat for animal feed is fuelled by animals needing to eat to survive. When purchases of animal meat increase, so do downstream sales of animal feed.
Food processing industries have ridden the wave of higher prices
•
Much demand comes from the Canadian Flour Milling industry (IBISWorld report 31121CA), which produces products that are then used as inputs into other foods like bread. Given the scale of food processing industries in other countries, a significant quantity of domestic wheat sent abroad is used for this purpose.
•
Due to recent population growth, there has been a greater need for processed foods. The sharp increase in the price of wheat in the past couple of years has also expanded the price per unit, which has driven a boost in revenue share for this segment.
Other markets comprise a modest share of revenue
•
This segment is comprised of other uses for wheat including sales of seeds by farmers to businesses or consumers and sales to industrial clients. Wheat products are widely used in industrial markets for things like wheat starch as an adhesive.
•
Due to the versatile nature of this segment, demand is driven by many factors. One factor is the level of manufacturing activity in Canada, which influences the amount of wheat required for producing consumer products.

What are the industry’s import trends?
The United States is the largest source of imports
•
The United States boasts a massive agricultural sector. Due to its significant production of wheat it exports a good share to Canadian businesses. The United States accounts for more than 90.0% of imports and is the only country that exceeds 3.0%.
•
The shared border and favourable trading status between Canada and the United States also incentivize trade. Imports of wheat from the United States have climbed in recent years as droughts in Canada have limited the domestic supply.
Imports account for a minimal share of domestic demand
•
Imports are not a significant aspect of wheat farming. In a given year, less than 2.0% of domestic demand is typically accounted for by imports.
•
Canada is the world's fifth largest producer of wheat, so there is little need to import from abroad. Canadian wheat production has also grown in recent years. Still, recent droughts have created uncertainty regarding the domestic supply as the population continues to grow.

What are the industry’s export trends?
Exports have grown amid soaring demand from Indonesia and China
•
Indonesia and China each account for a significant share of exports. When demand from these countries expands it dramatically changes operating conditions for Canadian wheat farmers.
•
When demand from a major export destination falls, it often leads to farmers facing intense domestic competition. This can erode profit as price competition becomes more popular.
•
Exports to China, in particular, have been a major source of growth in recent years. The value of wheat exports to China has expanded from just over $200.0 million in 2017 to more than $1.1 billion in 2023.
Asian countries provide a strong source of growth for wheat farmers
•
Demand for domestic wheat from Japan has also grown in recent years. The modest size of Japan limits its agricultural sector, making it more dependent on foreign trade.
•
China, Indonesia and Japan collectively account for about 30.0% of all exports. The United States is the only other country that consistently accounts for a sizeable share of exports.

Where are industry businesses located?
Production is focused in Saskatchewan and Alberta
•
Nearly three-quarters of domestic establishments are in Saskatchewan and Alberta, with Saskatchewan being home to most of the country's durum wheat production. Durum wheat grows well in Saskatchewan because of the rich soil and below-average rainfall.
•
Alberta contains a large share of Canada's winter wheat, as the climate provides a good environment for growth. The province also provides relatively easy transportation to large markets in Ontario, British Columbia and the United States.
Population does not dictate location
•
Unlike many other industries, wheat farmers are not typically located around population centres. Wheat production requires a lot of land, which can be cost prohibitive in more populated areas.
•
For example, Ontario, the most populated province in Canada, accounts for a very small share of wheat farm establishments. Quebec also has very few establishments.
Proximity to the United States and Asia provides a benefit
•
While farmers don't place a premium on being located near population centres, proximity to export markets is important. Exports account for more than half of industry revenue so it can be a significant advantage to be located near a port or border.
•
This is particularly important since the four biggest trade partners include the United States and three Asian countries. Cost is the major consumer consideration in export markets, just as in domestic markets.

Competition for suitable agricultural land can affect wheat farmers. There is only so much land available, so farmers may need to

Concentration Low
What impacts the industry’s market share concentration?
No farmer has a controlling share of the market
•
No single wheat farm controls more than 1.0% of revenue. Most farmers can run their farms without additional labour. In recent years, the advancement of farm machinery has further limited the need for workers at most farms.
•
Most Canadian wheat farms that employ workers employ fewer than five individuals. There is not a single wheat farm that employs more than 20 workers.
•
These factors limit the scope of any one wheat farm and prevents them from taking up any significant market share. This creates a fractured market with thousands of small companies that largely
operate at one location with few, if any, employees.
Wheat products don't have discernable differences
•
There is no clear differentiation between wheat grown by different farmers, making it difficult for any wheat farmer to obtain a significant share of revenue based on product mix.
•
There are differences in quality that determine the most applicable downstream use, though. Also, the growing use of genetically modified organisms (GMOs) has divided consumers. Some consumers aim to avoid consuming GMOs and are willing to pay a higher price for non-GMO products.

How do successful businesses handle concentration?
Adopt a high-volume, low-margin strategy
Wheat products don't have a very high profit margin per sale. Farmers can carve out a strong position in the market by focusing on selling a large quantity of wheat at a fairly low margin per sale.
Use automated procedures and processes
While farming still relies on manual labour, technological advancements have alleviated the burden to a degree. Farms can improve efficiency and production by investing in automated equipment. This can increase scale, which would aid in securing a larger share of the market.
Barriers to Entry Low Steady
What challenges do potential industry entrants face?
Wheat Farming in Canada
Legal
•
Wheat farmers must comply with all aspects of the Canada Grain Act, which is the legislative framework for Canada's grain quality assurance system. Farmers may be required to obtain licences, like a primary elevator licence.
Start-up Costs
•
Start-up costs vary depending on the scope of the farm. Farmers require land to begin operations, which is often the most significant start-up cost. Larger farmers need to invest in a tractor or harvester to efficiently work their land.
Differentiation
•
There is very little differentiation between wheat farmers. Differences in quality are largely the result of weather and other unpredictable events, making all farmers equally susceptible to a bad crop.
Labour Expenses
•
Canadian wheat farmers depend more heavily on capital than labour. Most than half of establishments are nonemployers, which severely limits the level of labour intensity. Farmers have also increasingly invested in newer, more costly farm equipment, further reducing the need for labour by many farmers.
Capital Expenses
•
While labour is important, farmers rely much more heavily on capital. Farmers have also increasingly invested in costly equipment, which has fuelled growth in capital spending, particularly when most farms have few, if any, employees.
 Key Success Factors
How can potential entrants overcome barriers to entry?
Have an exclusive sales contract
Most farmers have long-term contracts to downstream processors, including flour millers. This can help secure revenue when product differentiation is low.
Specialize in a product or service that is considered essential by legislators
As an integral part of the domestic food supply, wheat farming is considered essential by legislators. This enables farmers to continue operations largely uninhibited.
Substitutes Moderate Increasing
What are substitutes for industry services?
Imports
•
While Canada is a top-five global exporter of wheat, the United States accounts for an even larger share. This creates a relatively high degree of import competition for Canadian wheat farmers.
•
The United States routinely accounts for nearly all imports. Still, imports typically only account for about 1.0% of total domestic demand in a given year. This minimizes imports' overall impact on domestic farmers.
Alternate grains
•
Alternative grains to wheat, such as amaranth, buckwheat, millet, quinoa and teff, have grown in popularity in recent years. Previously, wheat was by far the most popular grain in the marketplace.
•
One reason for this has been a shift away from gluten-containing grains, which include wheat, barley and rye. Consumers have become more aware of gluten intolerances, while others believe it to be relatively unhealthy.
•
Despite gains made by other grains, wheat is still the primary grain used in many cereals, breads and pastas. Wheat is also still popular in animal feeds.

How do successful businesses compete with substitutes?
Keep running costs low
Wheat farmers have to contend with a relatively thin profit margin. Rising costs have forced farmers to be especially focused on limiting expenses to keep their own prices compeititve.

Larger wheat farms are more likely to grow other plants, including substitute grains. This will help these farmers better navigate changes in relative popularity of wheat, as well as their other crops.

What power do buyers and suppliers have over the industry?
Moderate Steady
Buyers: price competition
•
Since there is little differentiation among wheat products, price is the main competitive factor. Consumers will choose the lowest-priced product when all other factors are the same.
•
Wheat farmers attempt to keep prices fairly low to attract consumers, focusing more on a high sales volume. Prices are set based on global supply and demand conditions, which domestic farmers struggle to control.
Moderate Decreasing
Sellers: purchase costs
•
Wheat farmers use a wide variety of inputs like seeds, fertilizers and other chemicals. Farmers can attempt to lower their costs by negotiating prices with suppliers or switching suppliers.
•
Sometimes price negotiations can be difficult for farmers, as upstream suppliers have increasingly consolidated, limiting upstream competition. Purchase costs have grown steadily as a share of revenue in recent years, though they haven't grown as a share of revenue. Wheat farmers have successfully passed them on to consumers.

Regulation & Policy Moderate Steady
What regulations impact the industry?
Canadian Grain Commission
The Canadian Grain Commission (CGC) operates at the federal level and helps regulate the grain handling industry. Under the Canada Grain Act, the CGC sets and maintains the quality standards for 21 types of grain, including wheat. It also gives producers the right to order railway cars through the CGC to ship any designated grain and provides payment safeguards in the case of disputes. The CGC also provides research services.
Canadian Food Inspection Agency
The Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA) manages the safety of the domestic food supply. It also helps to administer and enforce the Food and Drugs Act, which governs the safety and quality of all food products sold in Canada. The Food and Drugs Act also regulates a wide range of food types like genetically modified goods and organic products. Only products that meet an organic content threshold greater than or equal to 95.0% may be labelled as “organic.”
Canadian Wheat Board
The Canadian Wheat Board (CWB) was the sole buyer and seller of wheat and barley in the prairie region of Canada. It was illegal for producers to sell grain to any operation other than the CWB. Government policy changed in 2012, making it no longer mandatory for producers to work through the CWB. In 2015, a majority stake in the CWB was purchased by G3 Global Grain Group and it turned the organization into a new company called G3 Canada Limited.

Government Canadian Federation of Agriculture
The Canadian Federation of Agriculture (CFA) is one of the largest domestic farmer organizations. It represents more than 200,000 Canadian farms of all sizes. The CFA promotes the interests of the domestic agricultural industry and its members include provincial general farm organizations, national or interprovincial commodity organizations and national or interprovincial producer-owned and controlled cooperatives.
Government Canada Grains Council
The Canadian Grains Council was formed in 1969 to assist the Canadian grain industry by increasing the sale and use of Canadian grain in domestic and world markets. The organization is composed of numerous types of companies across the sector, including producers, grain processors, end users, financial institutions and regulatory agencies.
Non-government Industry associations
The Western Canadian Wheat Growers Association was chartered in 1970 and is headquartered in Saskatchewan. The association has worked on a range of issues since being established, including the elimination of inter-provincial boundaries on feed grains, reform of the overall grain market and transportation infrastructure. Other national organizations include Grain Growers of Canada. There are also regional associations including Grain Farmers of Ontario and the Agricultural Producers Association of Saskatchewan.

What trends impact industry costs?
Wheat farmers can pass on purchase costs, keeping their share of revenue steady
•
Fertilizer and seed purchases are the largest contributors to purchase costs. Other purchases include chemicals and repairs and maintenance for machines and equipment like tractors and spreaders.
•
Wheat is a staple part of the diet for not only Canadian humans, but also many animals. This means farmers are often successful at passing on any increase in costs to downstream processors.
•
This keeps purchase costs steady for wheat farmers, even when fertilizers, other chemicals and seeds fluctuate in price. This also serves to protect profit from dipping too far when purchases are high.
Land ownership keeps rent costs low
•
Most wheat farmers own the land that they farm. Rent includes the
opportunity of using land for agricultural purposes, plus other equipment rental costs and rental of storage facilities for excess harvest product.
•
Rent costs also include the cost of renting machinery, which can be leased on an as-needed basis to keep depreciation charges low. Most other rental costs are minor.
Growing wheat prices help profit
•
Profit for wheat farmers is modest compared with other farming industries. Recent growth in wheat prices has enabled farmers to charge higher prices and boost profit. This expansion was largely the result of decreased global supply because of difficult growing conditions and the war in Ukraine. Prices have come down from their 2022 peak, but are still well above those from last decade.
•
The quality of Canadian wheat has also helped grow sales and profit. Wheat from Canada is sought after in global markets, allowing farmers to charge a premium.
Wheat Farming in Canada
•
Demand for wheat is largely inelastic. Since wheat is used widely in a variety of ways, demand rarely decreases. The biggest threat to wheat farmers is consumers switching to other grains, like rye and barley.
Investment in technology has tempered wage growth
•
Farmers have spent money to invest in machines and other technology to reduce their reliance on labour. This has kept wages from growing significantly as a share of revenue. Farmers have been able to keep wages relatively steady despite wage growth in nearly every other sector.
•
Examples of this labour-saving technology include upgraded combine harvesters, tractors and irrigation systems. Still, farming will continue to rely on manual labour moving forward for many tasks.

Wheat Farming in Canada
Key Success Factors
How do successful businesses overcome volatility? Ability to alter goods and services produced in favour of market conditions
Farmers with the ability to diversify their output between wheat and other types of crops will be more profitable and better able to withstand periods of poor market conditions for any single type of product. This would help combat revenue volatility.
Specialize in a product that is considered essential by legislators
As an integral part of the domestic food supply, wheat farming is considered essential by legislators. This has enabled farmers to continue operations largely uninhibited, which reduces disruptions in operations and cuts down on volatility.
What products or services do successful businesses offer? Alter goods and services produced in favor of market conditions
The ability to alter the balance between wheat and other crops or livestock production is a key factor for a farm's viability. Diversified farms are more profitable and are able to withstand downturns in the profitability of wheat.
Establish supply contracts for key inputs
Farmers endure significant volatility with input prices. As consolidation has become more common among upstream suppliers, many farmers have established supply contracts to avoid being price gouged because of volatility.
How do businesses use location to their advantage? Secure export markets
Export markets account for more than half of demand for Canadian wheat farmers and provide a far wider range of opportunities than the domestic market.
Appropriate physical growing conditions
Farmers must carefully choose what products to produce on their land to maximize yields and profitability. Certain products that are unsuitable for one region will thrive in another.
How do successful businesses handle concentration? Adopt a high-volume, low-margin strategy
Wheat products don't have a very high profit margin per sale. Farmers can carve out a strong position in the market by focusing on selling a large quantity of wheat at a fairly low margin per sale.
Use automated procedures and processes
While farming still relies on manual labour, technological advancements have alleviated the burden to a degree. Farms can improve efficiency and production by investing in automated equipment. This can increase scale, which would aid in securing a larger share of the market.
How can potential entrants overcome barriers to entry? Have an exclusive sales contract
Most farmers have long-term contracts to downstream processors, including flour millers. This can help secure revenue when product differentiation is low.

As an integral part of the domestic food supply, wheat farming is considered essential by legislators. This enables farmers to continue operations largely uninhibited.

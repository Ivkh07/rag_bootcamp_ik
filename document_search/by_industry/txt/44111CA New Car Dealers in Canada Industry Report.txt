Executive Summary New wheels: Interest rate hikes have mitigated strong revenue growth
Canadian new car dealers have endured volatile demand through the current period. During the pandemic, weak consumer confidence, disposable income and corporate profit led to sharp declines in 2020. Lockdown restrictions also limited driving and travel activity, contributing to weak demand for new cars and repair services. However, rising incomes and rebounding confidence have supported the industry's recovery in the following years. Similarly, torrid demand for driving and travel has led to strong growth from consumer and commercial markets following the pandemic, even as interest rates threaten stability. Overall, revenue has climbed at an expected CAGR of 2.8% to $190.6 billion through the current period, including a 3.9% jump in 2024, where profit reached 2.0%.
Supply chain disruptions have posed a major threat to new car dealers throughout the current period. Soaring input prices made new cars significantly more expensive, increasing inventory costs. Similarly, semiconductor and electronic component shortages reduced supply, leaving dealers with limited inventories. Even so, dealers could largely leverage torrid demand and pass added costs onto buyers, creating strong revenue growth. Companies have also integrated online services to make the car-buying process simpler and more accessible. Despite sharp declines during the pandemic and volatile supply chains, these systems have helped companies cut costs and salvage profit.
New car dealers will benefit from rebounding economic conditions through the outlook period. Strong consumer and commercial markets will provide steady demand and encourage buyers to trade up to more expensive, luxurious models. Similarly, upstream innovations and government programs will bolster demand for zero-emission vehicles (ZEVs), creating robust growth opportunities. Even so, labour shortages remain a major threat to profitability; a dearth of skilled automotive technicians may influence growth from repair segments. Overall, revenue will expand at an expected CAGR of 3.2% to $223.6 billion through the outlook, where profit will reach 2.0%.

What's driving current industry performance?
New car dealers have endured volatile conditions
•
The pandemic caused revenue to contract in 2020. Plummeting consumer and business confidence encouraged buyers to save, translating into weak new car sales. Similarly, stay-at-home orders slashed driving activity, limiting demand for dealer repair services.
•
In comparison, rebounding consumer confidence and strong corporate profit in the following years encouraged new car purchases, especially as individuals and households expressed pent-up demand for driving and travel. Mounting demand combined with higher prices contributed to robust revenue growth for dealers in Canada.
•
Similarly, inbound international travel skyrocketed in 2022, suggesting elevated demand for taxi, rideshare and rental car companies. This trend encouraged commercial buyers to expand and update vehicle fleets, contributing to strong demand and helping dealers diversify demand.
•
Even so, climbing interest rates have threatened sustainable growth. Elevated rates have made financing vehicle purchases more expensive, pushing some lower-income buyers out of the market. Regardless, pent-up demand for driving and travel, economic stimulus and rebounding consumer confidence have driven revenue growth through the end of the current period.
Supply chain disruptions have added to uncertainty
•
The pandemic and the Russian invasion of Ukraine created uncertainty across the automotive supply chain. Soaring oil and steel prices made most vehicle parts more expensive, significantly increasing inventory costs. Climbing oil prices also made operating combustion vehicles more expensive. This trend encouraged some individuals to prefer public transportation, though most Canadian provinces lack adequate public transit.
•
Similarly, massive semiconductor shortages left upstream automakers without key components, leading to longer lead times and severe inventory shortages for new car dealers. Most dealers were able to take advantage of torrid demand for new vehicles, raising prices and passing costs onto buyers. However, some dealers lacked suitable inventories and lost out on a portion of demand.
•
Despite higher costs, Canadian buyers were unable to switch to alternatives, like public transportation. According to management consulting firm Oliver Wyman, Canadian cities ranked poorly in terms of public transportation affordability, accessibility and sustainability. Only Vancouver, Montreal and Toronto displayed strong public transportation systems, highlighting the need for driving in Canada.
Dealers have expanded in-house financing services
•
Canadian new car dealers have sought to reduce their reliance on credit-issuing companies and instead bolster in-house financing

capabilities. Dealers also offer in-house financing options to cater to customers with poor credit ratings who commonly pay higher interest rates, increasing demand from lower-credit markets.
•
Dealers have broadened in-house financing from “buy here, pay here” (BHPH) arrangements to include “lease here, pay here” (LHPH), in which dealers maintain ownership of leased vehicles. Vehicles sold under LHPH add to depreciation expenses, reducing dealers' tax liability.
•
According to Automotive News Canada, consumers financed approximately 54.0% of new vehicle sales in 2023, generating additional long-term interest on most sales. Similarly, leasing has accounted for more than 20.0% of new vehicle sales, though leases have grown more expensive as interest rates rise.
Online dealer services have expanded
•
Websites and mobile apps have made browsing for new and used vehicles more convenient, driving vehicle sales. E-commerce’s proliferation has also increased competition for brick-and-mortar car dealers as consumers increasingly prefer completing some or all of their car purchase process online.
•
Various car evaluation services, like CarFax Canada, have led to a wave of more informed car buyers. These customers are more likely to explore prices and brands at different dealers and online retailers, fostering competition.
•
A strong online presence has become essential for dealers to remain competitive and tap into the growing market of digital-savvy customers. The pandemic also encouraged online alternatives; many dealers prioritized online expansion as lockdown restrictions limited in-person sales.
•
Even so, increased online capacity comes with potential drawbacks. Nearly all car dealerships in Canada and the United States use software from CDK Global; a June 2024 hack of this system has left dealers unable to process orders, manage inventories and complete many other simple tasks. Overall, the profit potential associated with new software has outweighed the risk presented by cybersecurity lapses.

Volatility Moderate
Economic uncertainty drags car sales down
•
Some consumers purchased vehicles to avoid public transportation during the pandemic. Still, soaring unemployment rates and stay-at-home orders kept more consumers away from new car lots, resulting in a net loss in 2020.
•
In 2022 and 2023, soaring overnight rates and plummeting consumer confidence counteracted torrid 2021 growth. High interest rates have prevented some lower-credit buyers from financing purchases, leading to net declines.
Supply chain uncertainty contributed to volatility
•
A semiconductor shortage slowed the global production of new vehicles in 2021 and 2022. Empty new car lots led to skyrocketing car prices, partially diminishing demand for new cars.
•
Volatile crude oil prices also altered vehicle consumption and use trends. High gasoline prices increased vehicle operating costs, encouraging greater use of public transportation and other substitutes. This trend has also contributed to greater demand for electric vehicles.
New technology will alter key processes
•
External technological innovations, like EVs, hybrids and autonomous driving, present potential challenges to new car dealers. Dealers have to keep up with these rapid changes and stocking vehicles that don't meet these expectations can hurt sales and cause revenue volatility.
•
Additionally, technological integration within the sales process itself, like digital transactions and virtual reality showrooms, demands that dealers adapt quickly. Those that don't might find themselves at a competitive disadvantage, reducing potential returns.

How do successful businesses overcome volatility?
Use of high volume/low margin strategy
Consumers have taken longer to purchase new cars, spending more time comparing prices between various online and in-person dealers. Dealerships that provide low prices will increase traffic and volume sales.
Superior financial management and debt management
New car dealers extensively use debt financing to purchase inventories, exposing them to considerable risk when the economy weakens. As new car sales drop, dealers must still pay interest on their debt-purchased inventory.
Having contacts within key markets
Dealers must have strong relationships with upstream automakers that supply vehicles and parts. These relationships can help dealers avoid supply chain volatility.
Outlook  2024-29 Revenue CAGR +3.2%
Improving economic conditions will support growth
•
Canadian new car dealers will benefit from strengthening economic conditions through the outlook period. Strong consumer confidence, per capita disposable income and consumer spending will encourage consumers to purchase new vehicles and trade up to more expensive brands. Dealers affiliated with luxury and other attractive brands may gain an extra advantage.
•
Similarly, strong corporate profit will encourage taxi, rideshare and rental companies to upgrade and expand existing vehicle fleets, especially as higher incomes encourage individuals to travel more.
•
Notably, interest rates will also recede through the outlook period, making vehicle financing more affordable, especially for lower-income buyers. This trend will spur new vehicle sales through the outlook period, creating favourable conditions for Canadian dealers.
Dealers will face prolonged employee shortages
•
The Canadian automotive care sector has faced major labour shortages as fewer students graduate with the necessary skills and licenses. The Automotive Industries Association of Canada (AIA) has reported a sharp decline in Automotive Apprenticeship Program completions and enrollments.
•
As a result, dealers will largely struggle to attract and retain skilled labour for the industry’s highly profitable parts and repair service segment. Wages will also swell as dealers, repair shops and similar automotive care industries fight for a limited labour supply.
•
Increased upstream innovation will also pressure the labour supply; electric and self-driving technology will require more technical and complex work, furthering wage growth. Companies may also need to shift sales and maintenance practices to adapt to the changing environment.
Zero-emission vehicles will create new opportunities
•
The development and advancement of electric and hybrid vehicles will drive sales through the outlook period. Significant improvements in fuel economy will encourage cost-conscious buyers to upgrade and reduce or entirely eliminate fuel costs.
•
New car dealers will also benefit from various government spending programs. For example, the Canadian government's Zero-Emissions Vehicles (iZEV) Program will provide various financial incentives to encourage consumers to buy zero-emission vehicles; similarly, the federal and provincial governments will continue to provide tax write-offs granted for businesses adopting hybrid and electric vehicles.
•
Canada has also heavily invested in EV charging infrastructure. For example, the government offers rebates up to 50.0% of the cost of home charging stations. Similarly, the Zero Emission Vehicle Infrastructure Program (ZEVIP) will aid in the proliferation of public charging stations.
•
The Canadian government has also set ZEV sales and production targets; according to Transportation Canada, 100.0% of new car sales must be zero-emission by 2035. This goal remains extremely ambitious given the current charging infrastructure, EV prices and limited EV profitability among manufacturers and dealers. This burgeoning segment will rely on charging availability and cost.
Substitutes will threaten sustainable growth
•
Public transportation will remain a long-term threat to new car sales. Major cities, like Toronto, Montreal and Vancouver, have relatively affordable and accessible public transit systems. This factor, combined with increased vehicle traffic, will encourage individuals to prioritize

•
In fact, more than 80.0% of Canada’s population lives in urban regions. Of this, approximately 70.0% have access to public transportation. However, less than 15.0% of the population uses public transportation to get to work or school, suggesting major blind spots in transportation infrastructure and highlighting the country’s continued reliance on vehicles.

Increased environmental awareness and cost-consciousness may also bolster interest in biking, motorcycling and public transportation. Biking has become an increasingly popular alternative to driving, but Canada’s cold, relatively harsh weather often limits biking to the summer months.
Life Cycle Mature
Contribution to GDP
New car dealers remain a strong contributor to the Canadian economy. Dealers are essential to the automotive supply chain, connecting manufacturers to consumers, especially since few automakers have developed successful direct-to-consumer sales models in Canada.
Market Saturation
The industry is highly saturated. Independent dealers and franchises primarily congregate in heavily populated regions, while only a few businesses operate in sparsely populated provinces. Online sales have enabled some dealers to permeate less saturated areas.
Innovation
New car dealers have displayed little innovation. Dealers primarily innovate by incorporating online marketing practices. Product innovations occur at the manufacturing level.
Consolidation
Dealers often merge to strengthen their control of local markets, gain regional market share or offer a more diverse inventory. Many dealers have also joined franchises to gain access to more resources and a stronger network. AutoCanada actively acquires competitors.
Technology & Systems
New dealers have incorporated point-of-sale, repair diagnostic and inventory management technologies. Online sales also present new opportunities for Canadian dealers.

Products and Services How are the industry’s products and services performing?
New vehicles have lost ground
•
New car sales account for more than a third of total revenue. New cars are more expensive than used cars and repairs. Consumers cyclically purchase new cars; in Canada, the average vehicle lifespan is 322,000 kilometres. Most consumers keep cars for 10 to 12 years.
•
Major innovations can encourage individuals to upgrade cars more frequently. People may trade in used vehicles for greater phone connectivity and safety improvements. Similarly, fuel efficiency afforded by hybrid and electric driving technology has encouraged many consumers to purchase new vehicles, supporting this segment.
•
This segment also relies on discretionary income and corporate profit. When economic conditions improve, consumers and businesses are more likely to purchase new vehicles. Dealers faced volatile new car sales through the current period; the pandemic curtailed discretionary spending in 2020, though near-zero interest rates and pent-up demand for travel caused strong growth in 2021.
•
Supply chain disruptions have also diminished new vehicle sales. Major semiconductor shortages led to long lead times among automakers, reducing the available stock of new vehicles. Many dealers leaned on used vehicles to sustain growth.
Used vehicles account for a growing share of the industry
•
New car dealers often buy used vehicles through auctions and customer trade-ins, re-selling them at a higher profit than new vehicles. Used vehicles are less expensive and more appealing to lower-income buyers.
•
While all used vehicle sales have increased amid new vehicle shortages, light-duty vehicle sales have skyrocketed, particularly as SUV popularity increases with households and families.
•
Used vehicles sold at new car dealerships often come with extended warranties or certificates of inspection, which enable new car dealers to capture additional parts and services revenue and higher selling prices. Vehicles under warranty can only be serviced at a specific dealership or franchise.
Parts and repair services generate strong returns
•
This segment includes revenue from selling parts and performing repair services, such as wheel realignment, fluid replacement and more substantial mechanical repairs. Parts and services performed well during the pandemic, as many consumers prioritized repairs over major purchases.
•
Many new car dealers offer warranties and other long-term service deals. This practice enables dealers to generate more repeat customers. Repair services often generate higher returns compared with vehicle sales.
Many buyers require financing and insurance services
•
This segment includes revenue from financing and insurance services. New car dealers frequently serve customers with average and poor credit that are often denied affordable interest rates on loans from mainstream financial institutions.
•
The “buy here, pay here” financing arrangement enables customers to make weekly or twice-weekly cash payments directly to the dealership. Finance and insurance services generate stable, long-term revenue.
•
Financing segments generate strong revenue for dealerships; this segment has expanded as consumers gain greater access to credit and vehicle ownership rates rise, pushing many buyers to purchase higher-end vehicles and lease cars.

What’s influencing demand from the industry’s markets?
Super-prime customers remain steady
•
This market includes super-prime customers (credit scores of 781 or higher). These customers have a strong history of on-time payments and use a small share of the credit available to them.
•
Super-prime customers historically drive purchases of more expensive and luxurious new cars. The super-prime market can finance a new vehicle and these buyers receive lower interest rates.
Greater access to credit boosts prime and near-prime customers
•
This market includes consumers with prime credit ratings (a VantageScore between 661 and 780) and near prime ratings (a VantageScore between 601 and 660). According to Experian, most consumers have credit scores between 600 and 750.
•
Expanding financial stability and literacy have encouraged more drivers with prime and near-prime credit scores to purchase used vehicles. Consumers in this segment and the subprime category have also benefited from looser financing standards and easier access to credit.
Subprime and deep subprime customers increase as the economy recovers
•
This market includes subprime customers (those with a VantageScore between 501 and 600) and deep subprime customers (those with a score of less than 500).
•
Subprime and deep subprime customers are the most likely to default on debts, especially during periods of high unemployment. Increased in-house financing options have made new car purchasing more affordable, increasing demand from low-credit markets.
•
COVID-19 and the associated economic downturn have discouraged drivers in these market segments from taking on the financial stress of buying a new car. However, economic stimulus and recovering disposable income have supported this segment.
Commercial buyers represent a growing market
•
Commercial buyers include wholesalers, other retailers for resale, farms, repair shops and businesses for end use. Common
commercial buyers include rental services, taxi and rideshare companies, restaurants and other delivery services. Other markets for new car dealers include government agencies, including local and federal law enforcement.
•
Corporate buyers often purchase vehicles through a separate fleet department within a dealership. Many larger businesses instead use a wholesale company or go directly to the manufacturer.
•
Rideshare and delivery services have grown more popular following the pandemic, boosting demand from this market. Similarly, inbound tourism has steadily increased following the pandemic, boosting demand for new vehicles from rental and taxi companies.

How do successful businesses handle concentration?

New car dealers are extensive users of debt capital, exposing them to considerable risk when the economy weakens. Dealers must pay interest on their debt-purchased inventory regardless of sales.
Barriers to Entry Moderate Increasing
What challenges do potential industry entrants face?
Legal
•
New car dealerships face various legal barriers. Many state and local governments require dealerships to obtain a license and comply with advertising, sales and financing regulations. In Ontario, new car dealers must pay into the Motor Vehicle Dealers Compensation Fund.
Start-Up Costs
•
New car dealerships face relatively low investment requirements, though storage locations and storefronts in high-traffic areas can increase costs. Dealers also require extensive capital to purchase vehicles and finance customers' purchases.
Differentiation
•
Entrants often struggle to differentiate from established dealerships. New companies must compete for relationships with suppliers, financing companies and customers. Diverse inventories, strong locations and superior after-sales services can also help entrants differentiate.
Labour Expenses
•
New car dealers are labour-intensive. Dealerships need salespeople, service technicians and administrative staff. Finding skilled salespeople and mechanics can pose a moderate barrier to entry.
 Key Success Factors

Provision of superior after-sales service

Dealers need strong relationships with upstream automakers that supply vehicles and parts. Strong relationships can decrease recurring inventory costs for dealers.

Regulation & Policy High Steady
What regulations impact the industry?
Motor Dealers Act
Canadian new car dealers must follow the Motor Dealers Act, which regulates the licensing of automotive dealers. This law sets dealer standards, including a requirement that dealers disclose a vehicle's history. Companies are also subject to lemon laws, which require a dealer to fix a defective vehicle under the warranty that was sold with the vehicle. This doesn't compel a dealer to refund or replace the vehicle unless it's outlined in the warranty.
Occupational regulations
Canada's Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) Regulations include guidelines for structures, electricity, sanitation and sound, among other topics. These rules also contain wage standards for retail employees, which industry operators must follow. Dealers also must be compliant with equal wage standards and tax laws.
Dealer certification
Potential entrants in most provinces, including British Columbia, Ontario and Saskatchewan, must complete and pass a certification course to become a dealer. Courses, which include information on the industry and how to maintain professionalism, typically take nearly 24 hours to complete. Additionally, all dealers must complete and pass a background check. Fraud convictions can preclude obtaining a dealership license.
Warranties and recall obligations
Dealers must honor any manufacturer warranties and notify customers promptly about recalls. This involves providing repair or replacement services for defective parts at no extra cost to the buyer. Ensuring compliance with warranty obligations maintains customer satisfaction and road safety.
Financing regulations
Financial institutions and car dealers must follow strict guidelines when offering loans or leasing vehicles. This includes disclosing all terms and conditions of a financial agreement upfront, such as interest rates, fees and repayment schedules. Transparency in financial dealings protects consumers from hidden costs and unfair lending practices.

Car dealers in Canada often don't receive direct government assistance. Few direct grants or subsidies are afforded to dealerships from both federal and provincial governments. By improving skills and knowledge, dealers can offer better customer service and optimize their operations.

What trends impact industry costs?
Dealers rely on skilled labour
•
Skilled labour is an essential aspect of the industry. According to the Canadian Automobile Dealers Association (CADA), dealers primarily employ sales and customer service workers alongside parts and maintenance employees. Companies also employ management, administrative and other service-related positions, but companies have automated many lower-skilled tasks to reduce wages.
•
Canadian dealers have dealt with severe labour shortages. According to the Automotive Industries Association of Canada (AIA), Canada's automotive care sector has struggled to hire and retain skilled labour. Automotive Apprenticeship Program completions have plummeted and jobs are becoming increasingly complex with the proliferation of electric vehicles.
•
Canadian new car dealerships must offer premium wages to attract and retain skilled labour, especially as the skill and technological gap widens. Companies must compete with similar industries, like used car dealers, car body shops and other automotive care
industries for a limited labour supply.
Profit varies among dealers
•
A company's operational scale influences profit generation. Larger companies benefit from lower operating expenses established through economies of scale. These companies often have more diverse inventories and direct sales agreements or contracts with automakers, reducing costs.
•
Profit also varies by a dealer's product and service mix. In general, parts and service sales, alongside financing and insurance activities, generate the widest returns. Dealers that can offer a wide range of products and services often generate more stable profit.
•
Even so, volatile car prices and severe economic uncertainty curtailed strong demand for driving and travel. The industry also primarily imports cars from foreign manufacturers, contributing to higher costs and lower profit.

Key Success Factors
How do successful businesses overcome volatility? Use of high volume/low margin strategy
Consumers have taken longer to purchase new cars, spending more time comparing prices between various online and in-person dealers. Dealerships that provide low prices will increase traffic and volume sales.
Superior financial management and debt management
New car dealers extensively use debt financing to purchase inventories, exposing them to considerable risk when the economy weakens. As new car sales drop, dealers must still pay interest on their debt-purchased inventory.
Having contacts within key markets
Dealers must have strong relationships with upstream automakers that supply vehicles and parts. These relationships can help dealers avoid supply chain volatility.
What products or services do successful businesses offer? Provision of superior after-sales service
Providing parts, repair services and vehicle trade-ins enhances revenue and profitability. High service standards are key to a dealer's success and create higher margins.
Alter goods and services produced in favor of market conditions
New car dealers must be aware of changing consumer preferences, particularly regarding size, emissions and other qualities and adapt inventories accordingly.
How do businesses use location to their advantage? Operate in a highly visible location
New car dealerships can benefit from easy visibility. Many dealerships operate on busy streets and employ flashy signs and other storefront advertisements to attract buyers.
Operate in a location that is close to key markets
Many new car dealers in Canada prioritize proximity to major markets in highly populated regions to gain access to a greater client pool.
How do successful businesses handle concentration? Provision of superior after-sales service
Dealerships need access to reliable maintenance facilities to service warranty agreements and offer higher levels of customer service over competitors.
Use of high volume/low margin strategy
Canadian dealers maintain thin margins; companies rely on strong inventory turnover across high inventory volumes to succeed. Larger companies find more success given economies of scale.
Superior financial management and debt management
New car dealers are extensive users of debt capital, exposing them to considerable risk when the economy weakens. Dealers must pay interest on their debt-purchased inventory regardless of sales.
How can potential entrants overcome barriers to entry? Provision of superior after-sales service
Providing parts, repair services and vehicle trade-ins enhances revenue and profitability. High service standards are key to a dealer's success and can help dealers build repeat customers.

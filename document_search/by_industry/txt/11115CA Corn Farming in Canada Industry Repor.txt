About This Industry
Definition Corn farmers primarily grow corn and produce corn seeds. Corn commonly refers to the grains or kernels of Zea mays, a tall annual cereal grass. Corn is a staple food in many parts of the world and is also a key input in the production of biofuel, sweeteners, oil and other products. Sweet corn is not included in this industry.

Executive Summary Grab some popcorn: Demand for corn used in ethanol production will likely rise as fossil fuel prices grow
The Canadian corn farming industry has navigated a landscape rife with both volatility and opportunities in recent years. Revenue grew modestly, influenced by factors like fluctuating corn prices and rising input costs. The industry's performance has been strongly impacted by demand from animal feed and human food products, alongside industrial applications like ethanol. Technological advancements such as precision agriculture are beginning to reshape farming practices, improving efficiency and sustainability. Overall, industry revenue has climbed at a CAGR of 0.9% to reach an expected $2.85 billion in 2024 after a decrease of 10.3% in the year.
Over the past five years, corn prices experienced notable increases, especially between 2021 and 2023, driven by global demand and high costs for inputs such as fertilizer and oil. In 2024, prices have dropped as these costs decrease, supply remains high and US corn prices fall, leaving a big dent in revenue and profit. These shifts have also slashed profit as farmers struggle to balance operating costs and long-term supply contracts with dropping prices. However, the industry has been buoyed by strong demand for corn, with a robust export market and heightened domestic demand for corn-based consumer products, such as corn syrup and whisky. The market for corn-based animal feed was also driven up by strong livestock production, although competition with other grains has been a consistent challenge.
The next five years promise a dynamic period for the corn farming industry. Consumer preferences are shifting toward gluten-free and plant-based products, creating greater demand for corn in diverse applications. The expansion of meat production will also sustain demand for corn as livestock feed, while decreasing ethanol production may redirect focus towards other industrial uses. Despite these opportunities, challenges linger. Climate change presents unpredictable weather patterns, necessitating adaptive agricultural practices and investments in resilient seed varieties. The price of corn will stay closely linked to global oil movements and competitive pressures, making it a volatile industry where price swings can significantly impact farmers' prospects. Overall, industry revenue is estimated to grow at a CAGR of 1.0% through 2029 to reach 3.0 billion.

What's driving current industry performance?
Price fluctuations have shaped corn farmers' success
•
The price of corn saw substantial increases in 2021 through 2022, driven by high demand and rising input costs such as fertilizer, which peaked in those years.
•
In 2023 and 2024, corn prices dropped significantly, impacting revenues and profit as market conditions adjusted after previous volatility.
•
High fertilizer and crude oil prices sharply raised purchase costs for farmers, leading them to push these costs on to consumers through

higher prices and lifting revenue. As the costs of these raw materials have begun to taper off, benefitting from repaired supply chains, the price of corn has suffered.
•
Price decreases in US corn, a major competitor, have forced Canadian crop farmers to lower their prices to compete.
Trade developments drive industry growth
•
Canadian export volumes have increased, thanks in part to top markets like Ireland, the US and the United Kingdom.
•
As the strength of the Canadian dollar dropped in 2022 and 2023, the price competitiveness of Canadian corn on the international market grew substantially. Its appreciation in 2024 has stopped this trend, but exports remain high.
•
Canada has bolstered its domestic supply through large corn imports, primarily from the US, which have offset the reduced carry-in stocks from previous years.
Consumer spending and changing demand impact the landscape of the industry
•
Rising consumer spending has increased demand for corn-based products like corn syrup and whisky, enhancing the grain segment's growth.
•
Industrial use of corn, specifically for domestic ethanol production, has not seen the same growth. There have been slight but steady declines in Canadian ethanol output, paired with increasing demand boosting the import of ethanol.
•
The national average yield for corn has improved, supporting stronger production that enables farmers to meet shifting consumer demand efficiently and at a competitive price point.
Animal feed demand supports consistent growth
•
Corn is a fundamental component in animal feed, driving substantial demand from livestock producers in Canada.
•
The rising domestic use of corn reflects increased demand from the animal feed sector, as corn's nutritional value makes it essential for livestock development.
•
The livestock industry's rapid expansion has led to higher feed prices, directly boosting revenue for corn producers and maintaining the industry's overall stability and growth.

Volatility Very High
Production volatility is caused by unpredictable weather and climate events
•
Crop yields by corn farmers are highly dependent on seasonal conditions. Plagues, droughts or severe weather phenomena can be a detriment to corn production.
•
Climate change is increasing the frequency of extreme weather events, which can greatly disrupt yields, reduce supply and raise prices both domestically and globally. As farmers in Canada and abroad contend with new unpredictable weather patterns, existing import and export balances have the potential to fluctuate with greater frequency.
Market price fluctuations in corn and competing grains create market instability
•
Global supply and demand shifts for corn and its substitutes, like barley, wheat and sorghum, cause frequent shifts in market prices. Unexpected changes in weather conditions or crop yields can lead to sudden price spikes or drops.
•
Consumer preferences and dietary trends may favor or sideline corn, impacting its demand unpredictably. Corn's market share can fluctuate as consumer tastes evolve, leading to revenue instability.
•
International trade policies, tariffs and agreements significantly influence corn prices and export volumes. Any changes in these regulations can quickly alter market access and pricing for Canadian farmers.

How do successful businesses overcome volatility?
Plant premium, disease-resistant crops
Growing premium, disease-resistant corn varieties reduces the impact of disease outbreaks and adverse weather, stabilizing yields. This approach lessens volatility by ensuring more consistent production, which helps mitigate market fluctuations.
Effectively manage risk
Implementing robust risk management strategies, such as crop insurance and financial hedging, helps farmers cushion against unpredictable factors like weather and price swings.

Evolving consumer tastes steer corn demand towards diverse and premium products
•
Consumers are leaning towards gluten-free food options, and there is increasing demand for corn as a substitute for wheat in bread and baked goods, pasta, cereals, snacks and more.

Anticipated increases in soybean planting could apply downward pressure on corn prices, but rising demand for corn as livestock feed will contribute to market stability.

Contribution to GDP
Industry value added (IVA) for Canadian corn farming has struggled with a highly volatile market, preventing it from growing in line with domestic GDP.
Market Saturation
Corn farming is at a point of high market saturation, with widespread market acceptance. Its use in the food, feed and biofuel sectors anchors demand yet limits growth potential.
Innovation
Incremental improvements are more common than groundbreaking changes in this industry. Innovations focus on enhancing crop resilience and yield rather than creating new demand through large product developments.
Consolidation
Consolidation is limited, with smaller farms largely remaining independent rather than merging with larger entities. Existing farmers are focused on maintaining their positions without significant shifts in market power.
Technology & Systems
The adoption of technologies, like precision farming, helps farmers modernize operations and boosts efficiency. Industry technology developments are centred on sustainability and productivity rather than on pioneering new markets.

Products and Services How are the industry’s products and services performing?
Corn for grain remains strong due to increased demand from animal feed
•
Corn for grain is primarily grown for use as a food product and animal feed.
•
This segment has experienced growth fuelled by heightened demand from the animal farming sector. The price of feed has also risen, contributing to increased revenue from corn for grain.
•
Corn is popular both as produce and as it is used in products such as corn syrup and whisky, which are experiencing increased demand due to rising consumer spending.
•
Ethanol production in Canada, a key use for corn, has been limited largely by production capacity, hindering the growth potential of this segment.
Import competition grows in animal feed markets
•
Corn for silage is grown mainly for livestock feed, particularly in the form of fermented fodder. Sales have likewise benefitted from strong pig, chicken and cattle farming industries.
•
Despite consistent production, the industry revenue share attributable to sales of corn for silage has been reduced due to the expanding demand for corn for grain.
•
The ongoing expansion of consumer markets for corn-derived products like ethanol and biofuels is diverting focus and resources towards grain, impacting silage demand.
Corn seeds constitute a modest portion of revenue
•
The remainder of production is made up of corn seeds for sowing purposes.
•
Farmers purchase new seeds annually, keeping segment revenue relatively steady. The demand for seeds remains consistent due to the necessity of maintaining crop cycles.
•
While seeds haven't experienced dramatic growth, they play a critical role in underpinning long-term industry stability and production continuity and their demand ebbs and flows with overall industry performance.

What products or services do successful businesses offer?
Alter goods and services produced in favor of market conditions
Successful farms have the ability to alter the balance between corn crops and other products in response to changes in market conditions for farm viability. Farmers need to be able to change their product mix to maximize farm returns.
Produce high value-added goods
Producing high-value-added goods can include growing organic corn or focusing on specialty varieties such as non-GMO or heirloom strains. These approaches allow farmers to meet niche consumer demands and access higher market prices.
Major Markets What’s influencing demand from the industry’s markets?
Demand from animal feed producers grows along with revenue from animal products
•
The animal feed market uses corn as a key ingredient in livestock diets. This segment makes up the largest market for Canadian corn. Corn competes with sorghum, barley, wheat and other grains in the animal feed market.
•
Increasing demand for chicken, pig, and beef cattle farming has elevated the need for corn feed, positively impacting revenues from this segment.
•
Strong wheat prices have incentivized many farmers to stick with or switch to corn, even as corn prices were elevated during the current period.
Demand for corn dips in biofuel markets despite increasing consumption
•
This segment includes industrial customers such as ethanol producers, who utilize corn for renewable energy, and farmers who purchase corn seeds for cultivation.
•
Despite increased ethanol consumption driven by Clean Fuel Regulations, domestic production has decreased, leading to a decrease in demand for corn as an input.
•
Rising imports emphasize the growing consumption gap, highlighting opportunities for domestic producers to potentially ramp up production to meet future demand.
•
Demand for seeds is growing in line with industry revenue, driven by the rising need for corn, which encourages more farmers to purchase corn seeds.
Corn's role expands in diverse food products
•
Corn serves as a vital input in manufacturing a wide variety of food and beverage products, such as sweeteners, bread, cereal and whisky.
•
Consumer concerns about health issues like obesity and cancer have dampened demand for corn-derived sweeteners, prompting the food and beverage industry to explore alternatives like cane sugar and noncaloric options.
•
Despite these concerns, corn's affordability has increased its use in fast food, fruit juice, yogurt, salad dressings and soups as producers focus on cost-saving measures. The popularity of corn as a food input has risen as a strategy to control expenses.
•
Consumer demand for organic and non-GMO corn products is rising as health-conscious customers seek alternatives that are perceived as healthier and more sustainable. Customers are often willing to pay a premium for these offerings, helping to grow revenue.

What are the industry’s import trends?
Domestic supply has been stretched thin by growing demand
•
Due to the high demand for corn products, domestic supplies have been heavily taxed in the current period, particularly as more corn is directed to exports. This has caused a sharp uptick in imports to satisfy the remaining domestic demand.
•
The United States accounts for more than 95.0% of imports. This trade relation is aided by the geographic proximity and favourable trading conditions of the United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement (USMCA).
Increasing domestic corn output is set to limit import levels
•
Rising corn production in 2023 and 2024 will help to limit Canada's need for imports, ensuring adequate supply for domestic use.
•
As climate events become more common both in Canada and abroad, having large domestic stores of corn helps to cushion large negative impacts on production without turning to other countries for supplies.

What are the industry’s export trends?
Demand from key international markets drives corn exports
•
Canadian corn is in demand in key international markets such as Ireland, the US and the United Kingdom. These markets account for a significant portion of Canadian corn exports, underscoring their importance for sustaining export growth.
•
Nearby countries like the US rely on corn to feed livestock and often don't have large enough production capacities of substitute grains to use when shortages occur, making imports of Canadian corn necessary.
Exchange rates affect Canada's corn export competitiveness
•
A weaker Canadian dollar can make Canadian corn more attractive to international buyers by lowering the effective cost. Conversely, a stronger Canadian dollar might dampen export demand, as it increases the price for foreign markets.
•
As the Canadian dollar depreciated in 2022 and 2023, exports were boosted, though this trend reversed in 2024.

Where are industry businesses located?
Ontario and Quebec dominate Canadian corn production
•
In both Ontario and Quebec, corn farms are located in the southernmost regions, adjacent to the US border, as the climate is most favourable for growing corn. Corn is best grown in temperate regions, leading most corn farmers to locate in areas that have plentiful sunshine and rain.
•
The proximity of these areas to trade hubs in the United States also incentivizes farmers to locate nearby. This proximity reduces transportation costs and improves product quality.
Corn production in other provinces is minimal
•
No other province accounts for more than 2.0% of establishments.
•
Other factors influencing geographic spread include proximity to processing plants, soil nutrition and flat terrain.
•
The geographic spread of corn farms fluctuates relatively little from year-to-year, although minor variations are possible based on changes in yields, weather patterns and returns in alternative agricultural industries.

What impacts the industry’s market share concentration?
Family-owned farms push down concentration
•
Corn farming, like much of the agricultural sector, is predominantly made up of small companies and family-owned farms that operate locally, focusing on niche market segments.
•
The industry's decentralized structure leads to a thinly distributed market share, limiting any single entity's ability to dominate and making it challenging for large corporate farms to consolidate power.
Geographical diversity limits centralized control
•
Canada's vast and varied geography results in different regions suited to corn farming, dispersing production geographically and preventing dominance by a few large players.
•
Climate variations across provinces create specific agricultural niches, encouraging a more balanced distribution of corn production throughout the country.
•
Regional crop rotation and environmental conditions necessitate diverse farming approaches, reducing the likelihood of market concentration.

How do successful businesses handle concentration?
Develop a clear market position
Establishing a distinct market position enables corn farmers to differentiate their products, attract unique customer segments and create brand loyalty, which is particularly beneficial in a low-concentration market where competition is more dispersed.
Use new technology to contain costs and boost productivity
Implementing the latest agricultural technologies and precision agriculture practices helps Canadian corn farmers enhance efficiency, reduce costs and improve yields, allowing them to better compete and thrive in a market with low concentration and diverse players.

Substitutes Moderate Increasing
What are substitutes for industry services?
Other grains
•
Corn competes with coarse grains, such as barley and sorghum, in the animal feed market. Losses in crop yields for one grain will boost sales of the others.
•
In the food market, corn competes with rice, soybeans, wheat and oats.
•
New comparable inputs for biofuel, including sorghum, are positioned to challenge corn in this rising market.
Imports
•
Price is the dominant basis of competition for corn. Foreign competitors will gain global market share if exchange rates, foreign supplies or poor growing conditions make Canadian corn too expensive. Trade agreements and tariffs also impact the feasibility of importing corn.
•
Imported corn provides a strategic alternative during domestic shortages, helping to stabilize prices and meet demand.

Regulation & Policy Moderate Increasing
What regulations impact the industry?
Canadian Grain Commission (CGC)
The CGC is an agency that acts as a scientific research organization on grain quality and a regulator of grain handling. Under the Canada Grain Act, the CGC sets and maintains the quality standards for 21 types of grain, including corn. The CGC also conducts scientific analyses of harvest and crop export quality via its Grain Research Laboratory and generates reports that offer information about the crop for each year.
Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA)
The CFIA, is responsible for the administration and enforcement of the Food and Drugs Act. The Food and Drugs Act regulates the sale of genetically modified foods. The CFIA also oversees the Organic Products Regulations. Under the Organic Products Regulations, only products with organic content that is greater than or equal to 95.0% may be labelled as organic. These products must be certified, and the name of the certification body must appear on the label.
Canada Grain Act
The Canada Grain Act regulates grain handling in Canada, ensuring fair market practices and upholding quality standards across various grains, including corn. For corn farmers, this framework establishes grading and quality requirements that influence how corn is evaluated, processed, and sold. By providing clear guidelines, the act supports product consistency and market credibility, shaping daily operations and contributing to the industry's long-term competitiveness.
Safe Food for Canadians Regulations
Introduced under the Safe Food for Canadians Act, these regulations target food safety nationwide. For corn producers, compliance means following strict protocols for growing, handling and transporting corn to prevent contamination. By adhering to these standards, corn farmers contribute to protecting public health and ensuring their products meet domestic and international market requirements.

What assistance is available to this industry?
Government Canadian Renewable Fuels Association (CRFA)
Founded in 1984, the CRFA promotes and advances the use of renewable fuels for transportation. The CRFA is made up of representatives from renewable fuel-producing companies, technology and equipment suppliers, car manufacturers, fuel technology providers, petroleum producers and feedstock suppliers.
Government Canadian Federation of Agriculture (CFA)
Founded in 1935, the CFA is the country's largest farmers' organization, representing more than 200,000 farmers and farm families. Among its members are provincial general farm organizations and inter-provincial commodity organizations from every province. The CFA promotes the interests of Canadian agriculture and food producers and assists members in forming and promoting national agricultural policies to meet changing domestic and international economic conditions.
Government Sustainable Canadian Agricultural Partnership (Sustainable CAP)
The Sustainable Canadian Agricultural Partnership is a $3.5-billion agreement launched in 2023 to enhance the competitiveness, innovation and resilience of the agriculture sector. This initiative provides $1 billion in federal programs and $2.5 billion in cost-shared programs with provinces and territories. For corn farmers, Sustainable CAP offers vital funding and support, fostering advancements in sustainable practices and technology adoption to maintain a robust and forward-thinking industry.
Non-government Canada Grains Council
Formed in 1969 to increase the sale and use of Canadian grain in domestic and world markets, the Canada Grains Council is an organization representing producers, crop input companies, grain companies, processors, end users, government departments, financial institutions, regulatory agencies and others. The council's mission is to advance the interests of grains, oilseeds, pulses and special crops sector in Canada.
Non-government Seed and fertilizer rebates from agribusinesses
Agribusiness companies often provide seed and fertilizer rebates to support corn farmers, reducing the cost of essential inputs and enhancing profitability. These partnerships allow farmers access to high-quality products at affordable prices, promoting advanced agricultural practices and improving overall farm efficiency.

What trends impact industry costs?
Volatile corn prices challenge profit stability in corn farming industry
•
Fluctuating demand for corn, especially from ethanol producers, has created inconsistent prices, affecting farmers' profit.
•
The surge in prices from 2021 to 2022, driven by increased demand for ethanol, limited stock and supply chain bottlenecks, provided strong but temporary profit boosts. However, the subsequent price declines in 2023 and 2024, amid recessionary fears, have put pressure on profit.
•
Competitive pressures from major corn-producing countries like China and Brazil further complicate profit stability, as domestic producers face challenges maintaining market share while contending with lower-priced imports.
Technological advancements drive up depreciation costs in corn farming industry
•
Investments in advanced machinery have increased, raising depreciation expenses over time.
•
The implementation of modernized infrastructure requires frequent upgrades, resulting in growing annual depreciation costs for farms.
•
As farms strive to maintain a competitive edge by adopting new technologies, they face accelerated wear and tear on equipment, elevating their long-term depreciation liabilities.
Rising crude oil and fertilizer costs drive up purchase expenses in corn farming
•
The surge in crude oil prices led to increased transportation and operational costs for corn producers.

Corn Farming in Canada
Key Success Factors
How do successful businesses overcome volatility? Plant premium, disease-resistant crops
Growing premium, disease-resistant corn varieties reduces the impact of disease outbreaks and adverse weather, stabilizing yields. This approach lessens volatility by ensuring more consistent production, which helps mitigate market fluctuations.
Effectively manage risk
Implementing robust risk management strategies, such as crop insurance and financial hedging, helps farmers cushion against unpredictable factors like weather and price swings.
What products or services do successful businesses offer? Alter goods and services produced in favor of market conditions
Successful farms have the ability to alter the balance between corn crops and other products in response to changes in market conditions for farm viability. Farmers need to be able to change their product mix to maximize farm returns.
Produce high value-added goods
Producing high-value-added goods can include growing organic corn or focusing on specialty varieties such as non-GMO or heirloom strains. These approaches allow farmers to meet niche consumer demands and access higher market prices.
How do businesses use location to their advantage? Operate in a location with appropriate water supply, soil type and climate
Selecting regions with optimal natural resources ensures high corn yields and quality. A good climate and healthy soil also help reduce dependence on fertilizer, pesticides, irrigation and other major expenses.
Ensure easy access to further appropriate land for development
Since farmers cannot significantly boost production without more land, gaining access to additional land offers the flexibility for farmers to expand operations and meet increasing customer demand.
How do successful businesses handle concentration? Develop a clear market position
Establishing a distinct market position enables corn farmers to differentiate their products, attract unique customer segments and create brand loyalty, which is particularly beneficial in a low-concentration market where competition is more dispersed.
Use new technology to contain costs and boost productivity
Implementing the latest agricultural technologies and precision agriculture practices helps Canadian corn farmers enhance efficiency, reduce costs and improve yields, allowing them to better compete and thrive in a market with low concentration and diverse players.
How can potential entrants overcome barriers to entry? Secure economies of scale
Achieving economies of scale allows Canadian corn farmers to lower production costs per unit by increasing output. This efficiency aids in overcoming capital-intensive barriers to entry, boost competitiveness and strengthen profit.
Develop strong working relationships with lenders
Establishing strong relationships with financial institutions provides corn farmers with access to crucial funding. This financial support is essential for overcoming barriers to entry by enabling investments in land, equipment and operations that are critical for successful farming ventures.
How do successful businesses compete with substitutes?
Corn Farming in Canada
Offer a competitively priced product
Maintaining attractive pricing is vital in combating substitutes like barley and sorghum. By optimizing costs and offering competitive prices, corn farmers can effectively retain market share and deter customers from shifting to alternative grains.
Conduct market research
Regular market research helps Canadian corn farmers understand consumer preferences and market trends, enabling informed decisions to remain competitive against substitutes. By anticipating shifts in demand, farmers can adjust production and prices to better meet market needs.
How do successful businesses manage buyer & supplier power? Establish supply contracts for key inputs
Securing long-term contracts with input suppliers helps corn farmers lock in stable prices and supply, mitigating supplier power. This stability ensures a consistent supply chain, enabling farmers to plan better while reducing dependency on fluctuating market conditions.
Secure export markets
By expanding into international markets, corn farmers can diversify their customer base, reducing dependence on domestic buyers. This diversification lowers buyer power, as farmers can mitigate domestic demand fluctuations by leaning more heavily on demand from other countries.

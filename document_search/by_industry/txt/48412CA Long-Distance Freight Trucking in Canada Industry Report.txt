Long-Distance Freight Trucking in Canada
About This Industry
Definition The Long-Distance Freight Trucking industry in Canada provides long-distance general freight trucking between metropolitan areas. The industry provides truckload services in addition to less-than-truckload services. The movement of specialized freight isn't included in this industry, though many operators generate marginal revenue through specialized freight activities.

Executive Summary Rolling along: Growth in consumer spending will lead to rising demand for freight transportation
Revenue for long-distance freight trucking services is expected to grow alongside favourable consumer trends. The pandemic era saw a heightened reliance on online shopping, leading to a rising number of parcel deliveries and bolstering revenue for trucking companies. Still, the period was marked by challenges like volatile diesel prices, which complicated pricing strategies and pressured trucking firms' competitiveness against rail distribution. The industry grappled with labor shortages as an aging workforce continued to retire and younger workers sought alternative careers, prompting companies to elevate wages to attract new drivers. Revenue is expected to grow at a CAGR of 1.0% to $31.3 billion through the end of 2024, including growth of 1.2% in 2024 alone.
Two significant trends include continued merger and acquisition activity and improved inventory control. The largest freight trucking companies have continued to acquire smaller companies, whether to enter new geographic markets or expand capacity. The proliferation of the just-in-time (JIT) inventory management system, which encourages manufacturers to purchase inputs when needed, helped freight trucking businesses as the JIT system caused manufacturers to demand more frequent shipments. Diesel prices remain a concern, affecting profitability and competitive dynamics between trucking and rail services. Labor shortages persist, necessitating competitive compensation packages to attract drivers and pressuring profit.
Revenue is expected to grow with aggregate consumer spending and total retail sales. A projected decline in diesel prices is anticipated to enhance profitability and boost competitive pricing, allowing trucking firms to compete more effectively against rail distribution. Companies are expected to invest heavily in environmentally friendly fleet options, including hybrid and electric vehicles, to mitigate fuel price volatility and respond to regulatory pressures. Technological innovations like autonomous driving and artificial intelligence are expected to continue being gradually adopted as they become more commercially viable, helping to address labor shortages. As the industry faces rising competition and technological investments, consolidation among major carriers is likely to rise, while new entrants are expected to continue entering the market. Revenue is set to expand by a CAGR of 1.8% to an estimated $34.2 billion through the end of 2029.

What's driving current industry performance?
Consumption levels are supporting revenue growth despite volatility
•
Resilient spending through most of the period supported the expansion of order volumes. A larger share of income going towards essentials like rent, healthcare and food soured consumer sentiment and reduced spending on other goods, which lowered growth for long-distance truckers late in the period.
•
The changing economic landscape from the pandemic era ultimately benefited freight trucking companies. As consumers became more accustomed to shopping for goods online, the number of parcels needing transport increased, driving up revenues.
•
The growing number of shipments requiring transport spurred the usage of nonemployer establishments looking to distribute cargo from ports and the US into Canadian markets. Consumers continuing to shop online and sourcing goods from global suppliers represented a significant portion of growth as truckers moved the incoming goods into warehouses.
Volatile diesel prices are causing mixed demand for long-distance trucking
•
Fuel surcharges are built into transportation contracts to offset volatile changes in fuel prices and create a degree of profit stability. Companies that don't establish fuel contracts are more susceptible to volatility in oil prices. Still, it's more difficult for freight companies to pass along price increases to consumers when oil and gas prices swing as dramatically as in recent years.
•
Rising fuel prices pressure the truckers' competitive advantage against long-distance railroad distribution. During periods of volatility, railroads often see a rise in usage from cost-conscious businesses looking to transport bulky cargo, limiting the truckers' revenue growth towards the end of the period.
•
Global production filling the supply gap is moderating diesel prices through 2024, providing relief for truckers, and lowering railways' advantage.
Labour shortages are continuing to impact the performance
•
An older generation of truckers has retired, and younger workers are less inclined to become truck drivers. Many young workers were drawn to similar-paying jobs that involve less strenuous and mobile lifestyles.
•
Companies need to offer competitive compensation to attract and retain drivers since stricter laws constrain the legal limit of driving hours. Companies are offering increasingly high wages to attract a new generation of drivers.
•
Growth remains contingent on companies' ability to secure a skilled and loyal workforce, which is posing a growing challenge as businesses continue to navigate through an aging workforce.

Previous supply chain challenges are prompting improvements in distribution
•
A growing number of businesses are tailoring their operations to meet the evolving landscape of supply chain solutions, which continue to become more integrated.
•
Businesses are investing in niche specialization and intermodal solutions that combine their operations with the existing infrastructure of other industries, particularly rail, to provide more efficient distribution solutions for clients and lower operational costs.
•
Less-than-truckload (LTL) services are seeing the largest amount of investment as businesses cater to the expanding e-commerce segment. As merchants continue to source goods from global markets to satisfy domestic demand, many businesses are contracting long-distance LTL services for distribution, which allows them to save costs and remain competitive in the online marketplace.

Volatility Moderate
Gas prices greatly affect trucking services
•
Revenue and demand are closely linked to international trade volumes, manufacturing, wholesaling and retail activity. These values can change significantly in a short period as they depend on consumer sentiment and spending. Fluctuating commodity prices directly impact revenue, as changes in fuel costs are often passed down to consumers. Commodity prices also affect demand for freight trucking, as some extraction companies rely on trucking to transport cargo to end markets.
•
As fuel prices rise, companies often impose fuel surcharges to boost revenue. Still, the competitive pricing landscape prevents them from fully passing on rising costs, limiting truckers' ability to benefit from fuel spikes.
Driver shortage continues to threaten operations
•
A driver shortage continues to cause volatility for trucking businesses. The retiring workforce pressures employers to hire and train new employees who demand higher wages and improved work-life balance, leading to fewer hours on the road and declining revenue per employee.
•
Businesses continue to address the shortage of drivers by rolling out programs to incentivize new hires, including enlarged bonus packages, improved benefits and shorter shifts.

The rebound in economic growth is expected to support revenue expansion
•
Consumer spending is expected to jump, prompting manufacturers to ramp up production to meet demand for various goods, necessitating freight transportation services to deliver inputs.
•
Rising private and construction investment levels will drive the need for freight transportation services to transport equipment and materials to various parts of the country to complete the construction of new infrastructure.
•
An expected decline in diesel prices will positively impact profit because of heightened levels of economic activity. Freight trucking businesses will leverage lower fuel costs to make their services more attractive to downstream markets by offering competitive pricing.
•
More pronounced economies of scale allow Class I railways to deliver a lower per-unit cost when distributing large cargo loads, allowing them to secure business during high energy prices. A moderation in diesel prices is expected to support truckers' ability to compete against railroad distribution, which tends to outperform during rising oil prices.
Investment into technological advances and electric vehicles is expected to continue
•
Freight trucking companies will aim to acquire hybrid, battery and plug-in electric vehicles to advertise a more environmentally friendly fleet and remove the uncertainty of fuel prices and unfavorable regulations targeting fuel efficiency in vehicles.
•
Introducing autonomous or semi-autonomous trucks will help mitigate the influence of labour shortages. Still, these technologies are not yet commercially viable, providing little relief for freight trucking companies in the short term.
•
Internet of Things and artificial intelligence technologies will continue to optimize route planning, provide improved transport analytics and offer more insight into the data collected by sensors on trucks, improving the efficiency and safety of the existing fleets.
New entrants into the market will continue spurring competition
•
Rising competition, combined with customers' increasing preference to outsource their logistics operations, will encourage further consolidation among the industry's largest carriers. Competition will continue to intensify as investment in technology becomes more important, giving established carriers an advantage.
•
Companies with a greater geographic reach will find it easier to win contracts. Additionally, customers who outsource their logistics operations will find it costly to switch carriers, benefiting large companies with the scope to fulfill large order volumes.
•
While consolidation activity will likely limit participation growth, the industry's low barriers to entry and strengthening demand for freight trucking services will encourage a significant number of entrants, particularly nonemployers, who are likely to be temporary hires as companies will look to manage the labour shortage.
Safety measures are set to continue being the priority
•
Freight carriers will increasingly focus on safety to ensure drivers' health and limit operational losses. Technology has provided a variety of improvements aimed at ensuring vehicles don't veer off the road, accelerate too quickly or travel down steep grades at excessive speeds.
•
The Canadian government's economic policies will favour freight transportation services. Investment in infrastructure will support demand for freight trucking and the safety of drivers, as roads and bridges are in better condition to facilitate cargo distribution.
•
Companies are expected to continue rolling out new technology like sensors and upgrade their fleets to collect more data on driving patterns and prevent potential accidents. Rising oversight over truckers' day-to-day driving is expected to prevent accidents and support the sound delivery of cargo.

Products and Services How are the industry’s products and services performing?
Truckload carriers comprise the largest share of revenue
•
Truckload (TL) carriers are dedicated entirely to one customer and make door-to-door deliveries of goods, making this segment less labour-intensive than less-than-truckload (LTL). Companies don't need intermediate freight-consolidation facilities, leading to low start-up costs.
•
TL shipping involves homogeneous cargo, dividing it into individual containers that are then shipped via LTL when they arrive at a distribution centre or warehouse.
•
The largest truckload carriers generally have low profits and generate less revenue per ton than LTL carriers. TL carriers are being constrained by higher fuel costs because surcharges are insufficient to fully offset the crude oil price fluctuations.
Consolidation of shipments supports the less-than-truckload (LTL) carriers
•
LTL carriers take partial loads from multiple customers on a single truck and then route the goods through terminals where freight is transferred to other trucks with similar destinations, making this segment more labour-intensive.
•
Rising costs elevated investment in computer systems like GPS and order-management systems that coordinate and plan shipping freight quicker and more accurately.
•
The expanding presence of e-commerce and more merchants requiring order fulfillment continue to drive gains in the LTL segment as smaller establishments benefit from cheaper shipments.
Trucking companies expand value-added services to boost their presence in the global supply chain
•
Other transportation services include value-added services like door-to-door transportation, freight transportation arrangement and customs brokerage, packing, logistics consultation services and other related activities. Additional services include warehousing, distribution, repacking, storage and freight forwarding. The product line shrank because of heavy investment in LTL and TL.
•
Trucking companies expanded their range of services to secure their place in global supply chains and grow revenue. Freight brokerage services are becoming more popular because of operators' investments in online marketplaces, which support smaller establishments and the presence of nonemployers that fulfill contracts.

What are innovations in industry products and services? Moderate
Intelligent machines and the Internet of Things boost efficiency
•
Artificial intelligence systems help clean and analyze big data collected in logistics operations, allowing for forecasting highly accurate outlooks for shipping volumes and proactively optimizing delivery schedules.
•
The Internet of Things optimizes route planning and improves estimates of delivery times. Growing connectivity helped companies better analyze optimal routes and ensure driver safety.
Growing investment in automation and electric vehicles
•
Fleet electrification continues taking center stage amid heavy investment in electric trucks. Still, the long-truck segment doesn't transition to EVs as quickly as other last-mile delivery sectors because of the industry's long-distance nature, which requires strong vehicle output. The EV infrastructure remains undeveloped for the needs of mass distribution.
•
Adopting cloud computing and AI is expected to streamline business processes and support back-office operations. The growing implementation of technology is expected to streamline cost structures, though concerns over safety and reliability persist.
 Key Success Factors
What products or services do successful businesses offer?
Offer a competitively priced product
Competitive pricing is essential in the Canadian freight trucking industry. By offering reasonable rates, companies can attract more clientele while maintaining profitability, especially in a market where cost efficiency is a critical factor for customers choosing service providers.
Adopt a high-volume, low-margin strategy
Moving large volumes of goods at competitive rates can drive success. The strategy helps companies capture market share, capitalize on economies of scale and optimize route efficiency, maximizing overall profitability.

What’s influencing demand from the industry’s markets?
Freight trucking companies focus on the domestic market
•
The domestic market for long-distance freight trucking, which includes both interprovincial and import transportation, accounts for the majority of revenue. The segment includes manufacturers, retailers, wholesalers, government agencies and construction companies.
•
The aggregate expansion in economic activity corresponds to rising demand for goods, boosting domestic freight transportation services. Recent growth in consumer spending and per capita disposable income supported the revenue expansion.
Foreign market is reliant on demand from the US
•
The foreign market segment comprises foreign companies importing trucked goods from Canada. The vast majority of clients in this segment are companies in the US, many of which import goods from their Canadian subsidiaries.
•
Growth in the market lagged behind growth in other markets in response to a growing emphasis on domestically sourced supply chains following COVID-19. Still, the proximity between Canada and the US continued to facilitate a substantial volume of trade, which remained steady despite other deepened focus on domestic operations.
The internal market comprises a modest share of revenue
•
The segment includes other companies in the Canadian transportation sector. Freight trucking companies often hire other long-distance trucking companies when they can't fulfill orders or
don't wish to spend capital on additional equipment or vehicles.
•
Incumbent enterprises often outsource contracts to nonemployers to fulfill distribution. The segment remained relatively steady as a share of revenue despite the labor shortage as current drivers stepped in to cover shifts in demand.

Where are industry businesses located?
Proximity to nearby markets is vital
•
Canadian long-distance freight trucking companies want to locate near domestic population centres and key markets in the United States. Locating near these vital markets lowers transportation costs which directly improves profitability for freight trucking companies.
•
The importance of US markets is confirmed by the concentration of establishments in Ontario. Meanwhile, Quebec, which contains more than 20.0% of the Canadian population, accounts for less than 10.0% of establishments.
Trucking companies avoid rural areas
•
Rural areas have a limited number of nearby downstream consumers, making these locations unsuitable for freight trucking companies. Still, some companies locate in these areas in an effort
to establish a niche and dominate the freight transportation activity in that area.
•
Manitoba is the only province with less than 5.0% of the total population that has a sizeable share of establishments. Manitoba's proximity to key markets in the United States attracts some freight trucking companies.
Ontario contains the majority of establishments
•
Ontario is the nation's most heavily populated province and is home to Canada's largest city, Toronto, in addition to its capital, Ottawa. A significant portion of the country's economic activity takes place in the province, requiring carriers to provide transportation of goods.

What impacts the industry’s market share concentration?
Low capital requirements are keeping the market competitive
•
Long-distance freight carriers have low barriers to entry. An individual can enter the industry with a commercial driver's license, a few basic tools and a truck. Entrants can often secure a loan to purchase a vehicle, which is the largest entry-cost.
•
Owner-operators lead the freight trucking industry, with over 60.0% of all establishments being nonemployers. Since 2018, the industry's low barriers to entry have resulted in the addition of more than 5,000 nonemployer establishments.
•
The presence of freight brokerages and agencies facilitating contracts with clients, support the entry of nonemployers, keeping the concentration low.
The brand image and reputation benefit incumbent enterprises
•
The largest freight trucking companies maintain substantial market power by securing large contracts with major manufacturers and retailers. Their ability to offer a diverse range of services gives them a competitive advantage.
•
Large companies determine rates and standards and maintain market dominance by outsourcing contracts to smaller competitors

How do successful businesses handle concentration?
Optimize operating capacity
Efficient capacity use is crucial in long-distance freight trucking. By maximizing loads and minimizing empty miles, companies reduce costs and improve profitability, enabling them to compete effectively in Canada's competitive market.
Ensure skilled labor is available
Skilled drivers and logistics personnel are vital for timely and safe deliveries. By ensuring a competent workforce, trucking companies maintain service quality and customer satisfaction, essential for standing out in a competitive environment.
Barriers to Entry Low Steady
What challenges do potential industry entrants face?
Legal
•
Prospective entrants must obtain a commercial driver's license (CDL) and register with the relevant government agencies. Freight trucking service providers must also adhere to standards set by Canada's Transportation Act Review.
Start-up Costs
•
Freight trucking providers incur the costs associated with obtaining a CDL, registering their truck and purchasing a truck. Start-up requirements are moderate.
Differentiation
•
A freight trucking company can differentiate itself through reputation and price. Reputation is largely based on prior performance records and the equipment used by a company. Carriers that can slash costs and provide a lower price will win business.
Labour Expenses
•
Labour is the biggest factor of success in this industry. Truck drivers have become harder to find which has pushed up labour costs in recent years.

How can potential entrants overcome barriers to entry?
Develop strong technical product knowledge
Understanding technology and delivery equipment enhances service efficiency and reliability. Truckers that invest in technical knowledge can optimize operations, reduce errors and provide superior service, making it easier to establish a foothold in the market.
Develop contacts within key markets
Building relationships with businesses and potential clients is essential. These contacts can lead to partnerships and contracts, fostering growth and helping truckers establish themselves in the industry by securing a steady stream of distribution opportunities.

Rail Transportation
•
Oil price volatility supports rail transportation because of developed economies of scale, which allows rail operators to distribute large volumes of cargo at lower per-unit costs than trucks, particularly over long distances.

Air cargo consolidators can provide bidirectional links among many locations, helping cargo move quickly and frequently as long as there's sufficient runway infrastructure. What power do buyers and suppliers have over the industry?
Moderate Increasing
Buyers: Shipping alternatives
•
Buyers primarily select the services with the expectation of delivery in full and on time (DIFOT). Ensuring prompt delivery time is essential to winning repeat business in a fiercely competitive industry.
•
Buyers can measure service quality by the frequency of service and capacity. It's relatively easy for buyers to compare freight trucking companies and choose the most reliable businesses for transport.
•
Many shipping alternatives within the industry and from alternative modes of transport, including rail and air shipping, provide truckers with many sources of competition, which benefits buyers.
High Increasing
Suppliers: Fuel prices and input components
•
Long-distance freight trucking companies buy bulk fuel and have storage tanks to mitigate fuel price fluctuations. Significant volatility in fuel prices can be difficult to pass along to consumers through surcharges, pressuring profit. A prolonged rise in fuel prices benefits the distribution through railroads, which operate at more pronounced economies of scale.
•
The trucking industry depends on securing the latest technology to comply with fuel emissions regulations and other safety requirements. Suppliers hold significant power when pricing new products, particularly amid rising regulatory scrutiny and the evolving technological landscape in the logistics industry.

Regulation & Policy Moderate Increasing
What regulations impact the industry?
Canada's Transportation Act Review
Canada's Transportation Act impacts long-distance freight truckers by establishing a framework that promotes safety, competition and sustainability initiatives. The Act ensures fair competition and prevents monopolistic behavior by incumbent enterprises, allowing smaller trucking companies to compete in the market. The Act emphasizes environmental sustainability, encouraging the adoption of fuel-efficient technologies and optimizing operations to curb carbon emissions.
National Safety Code
Safety is becoming a growing concern among legislators and freight trucking service providers. The National Safety Code encourages trucking safety, promotes efficiency and establishes consistent safety standards across the provinces and territories. The code covers safety and administrative regulations for single-driver licenses, knowledge and performance tests, driver examiner training, self-certification and on-road inspections.
Canadian Environmental Protection Act (CEPA)
The CEPA impacts long-distance freight trucking in Canada by enforcing emissions standards for pollutants like nitrogen oxide (NOx) and particulate matter (PM). Under CEPA, the Heavy-duty Vehicle and Engine Greenhouse Gas Emission Regulations set limits for CO2 emissions, pushing manufacturers towards fuel-efficient and alternative fuel technologies. CEPA requires period emissions testing to ensure compliance. It prompts freight and logistics businesses to optimize operations and reduce unnecessary mileage and emissions, aligning the industry with global sustainability trends.
Hours of service regulations
These standards determine the maximum time a driver may be on duty. They also set minimum rest periods for each day and for each period of seven, eight or more consecutive days of driving. While these regulations have historically been harmonized across Canada and the US, recent changes in Canadian regulations have created minor differences between the two nations' policies.

What trends impact industry costs?
Labour shortage continues pushing up wage costs
•
Most employees are truck drivers, though many companies employ workers for freight handling, packaging and distribution. As fewer people seek truck driving jobs, wages have grown to retain staff.
•
Wages have grown as a share of revenue in recent years as companies have struggled to fill a growing number of vacancies. Wage growth is expected to persist moving forward as the number of truckers continues to fall.
Capital expenditures keep purchases stable
•
The largest component of purchase expense is fuel. Other purchases include the maintenance cost of equipment, the purchase of replacement tires and provisions for amenities for truck drivers.
•
Though the price of fuel is volatile, freight trucking service providers often implement fuel surcharges when the price rises
above a certain level. These surcharges can protect profit to some extent by generating additional revenue to offset rising purchase costs.
•
Competition and the strength of demand can limit a carrier's ability to impose surcharges. Other major purchases include transportation services, vehicle maintenance and repair and leasing of equipment.
Wage growth and rising fuel prices pressure profit
•
While revenue has benefited from a growing retail sector, profit was pressured by rising costs. Fuel prices shot up significantly in 2021, with the global diesel shortage being an especially detrimental development for freight trucking providers.
•
Growing wage costs have contributed to the decline in profit as companies struggled to secure quality drivers. Since the industry has a high level of internal competition, companies must compete on the basis of price to attract and retain customers.

Long-Distance Freight Trucking in Canada
Key Success Factors
How do successful businesses overcome volatility? Establish long-term working relationships with raw material suppliers
Building strong ties with suppliers ensures a reliable flow of materials and components, reducing the impact of market disruptions and price spikes while enhancing operational stability and planning for freight companies in Canada.
Optimize operating capacity
Efficiently managing and utilizing fleet resources allows trucking companies to adapt to fluctuating demand, ensuring cost-effective operations and maintaining competitiveness during periods of volatility in the freight industry.
What products or services do successful businesses offer? Offer a competitively priced product
Competitive pricing is essential in the Canadian freight trucking industry. By offering reasonable rates, companies can attract more clientele while maintaining profitability, especially in a market where cost efficiency is a critical factor for customers choosing service providers.
Adopt a high-volume, low-margin strategy
Moving large volumes of goods at competitive rates can drive success. The strategy helps companies capture market share, capitalize on economies of scale and optimize route efficiency, maximizing overall profitability.
How do businesses use location to their advantage? Ensure skilled labor is available
Freight trucking providers have struggled to attract and retain skilled drivers in recent years. Younger workers have been reluctant to take positions that require significant time away from home without a significant hike in pay.
Operate in a location that is close to key markets
Freight carriers that are located near key markets in Canada and the US will have a significant advantage over the competition. Proximity to key markets limits transportation costs and ensures quicker delivery times.
How do successful businesses handle concentration? Optimize operating capacity
Efficient capacity use is crucial in long-distance freight trucking. By maximizing loads and minimizing empty miles, companies reduce costs and improve profitability, enabling them to compete effectively in Canada's competitive market.
Ensure skilled labor is available
Skilled drivers and logistics personnel are vital for timely and safe deliveries. By ensuring a competent workforce, trucking companies maintain service quality and customer satisfaction, essential for standing out in a competitive environment.
How can potential entrants overcome barriers to entry? Develop strong technical product knowledge
Understanding technology and delivery equipment enhances service efficiency and reliability. Truckers that invest in technical knowledge can optimize operations, reduce errors and provide superior service, making it easier to establish a foothold in the market.
Develop contacts within key markets
Building relationships with businesses and potential clients is essential. These contacts can lead to partnerships and contracts, fostering growth and helping truckers establish themselves in the industry by securing a steady stream of distribution opportunities.
How do successful businesses compete with substitutes? Ensure appropriate pricing policy

Effective cost management through pricing, including fuel surcharges, can help long-distance freight providers maintain profit. Freight trucking companies that don't establish contracts with suppliers can be exposed to significant volatility.
Secure a highly skilled workforce
Freight trucking companies rely on skilled and experienced labour to ensure prompt transportation. Retaining a skilled pool of drivers has become more difficult in recent years.
How do successful businesses manage buyer & supplier power? Have a diverse range of clients
Serving a wide variety of clients lowers dependency on a single market segment, reducing the influence of any single buyer. Diversification helps balance power dynamics and ensures stable revenue streams.
Develop a strong market profile
Establishing a strong market presence enhances brand recognition and customer loyalty. It reduces buyer power by making the service a preferred choice, lessening the impact of competitive offers in the market.
